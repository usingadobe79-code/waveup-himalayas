Choose Annapurna Base Camp (ABC) if you have 7-10 days, a tighter budget or no high-altitude experience. Choose Everest Base Camp (EBC) if you have 12-14 days, want the Everest experience and will build in acclimatization days. ABC tops out at 4,130 m; EBC reaches 5,364 m, and Kala Patthar, the best Everest viewpoint, is at 5,545 m.

The **Annapurna Base Camp vs Everest Base Camp** question is the one we hear most at WaveUp Himalayas. Both treks are classics, both end in the high mountains, and both are doable for fit beginners with the right preparation. But they differ a lot in altitude, duration, cost, logistics and atmosphere.

In this 2027 guide, we compare the **ABC vs EBC trek** on difficulty, altitude sickness risk, cost, permits, scenery, crowds and the best time to go. We also cover what most comparisons skip: where you actually sleep at each base camp, why the two treks are hard in different ways, how Lukla flights affect your schedule, and what is really included when you compare prices. By the end, you will know which **base camp trek in Nepal** is right for you. This guide comes from the WaveUp Himalayas team in Thamel, Kathmandu.

## ABC vs EBC at a glance

ABC is shorter, lower and cheaper; EBC is longer, higher and more iconic. Here is the quick comparison:

|  | Annapurna Base Camp (ABC) | Everest Base Camp (EBC) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Highest point | \~4,130 m | \~5,364 m (Kala Patthar \~5,545 m) |
| Typical duration | 7-10 days | 12-14 days |
| Starting point | Road transfer from Pokhara | Flight from Kathmandu to Lukla |
| Difficulty | Moderate, with long stone staircases | Moderate to hard, mainly due to altitude and length |
| Altitude sickness risk | Moderate | Higher |
| Scenery | Green valleys, rhododendron forest, a ring of peaks | High-alpine Khumbu valley, glaciers, Sherpa villages |
| Cost | Lower | Higher (Lukla flights, longer trip) |
| Best for | First-timers, short holidays, tighter budgets | Bucket-list trekkers with about two weeks |

## Annapurna Base Camp trek: the amphitheatre of peaks

![Annapurna Base Camp trek](/images/blog/AnnapurnaBaseCampTrekkingMulti-dayMountainGlacierTour-001.jpg "Annapurna Base Camp trek")

The [Annapurna Base Camp trek](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/annapurna-base-camp-trek/) is the best short Himalayan trek for most first-timers. It takes you from the lakeside city of Pokhara into the Annapurna Sanctuary, a high glacial basin ringed by Annapurna I, Annapurna South, Hiunchuli, Machapuchare (Fishtail) and Gangapurna.

The route climbs through terraced farmland, Gurung villages such as Ghandruk and Chhomrong, bamboo and rhododendron forest, and finally an open alpine landscape. Because the trail is lower and the days are shorter, ABC is popular as a **short trek in Nepal for beginners**.

**Why trekkers choose ABC**

- A shorter trip: the classic route fits in about 7-10 days
- No flight to the trailhead, so there is no Lukla weather risk
- Lower altitude, with a high point of about 4,130 m
- Lush, varied scenery, especially in spring when the rhododendrons bloom
- Lower overall cost than EBC

**What to know**

- The trail has thousands of stone steps, so strong legs matter more than lung capacity
- The sanctuary is a narrow valley prone to avalanches in heavy snow, so winter conditions need careful guidance
- Teahouses are simpler and more basic at the top

## Everest Base Camp trek: walking in the shadow of the world's highest peak

![Everest Base Camp trek](/images/blog/Old-Everest-Base-Camp-Trek-001.webp "Everest Base Camp trek")

The [Everest Base Camp trek](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/everest-base-camp-trek/) is the most famous trek in Nepal for a reason. You fly into Lukla, follow the Dudh Koshi valley through Sherpa villages like Namche Bazaar, Tengboche and Dingboche, and climb to the foot of the Khumbu Icefall at about 5,364 m. Most trekkers also hike Kala Patthar (about 5,545 m) for the best views of Everest, Nuptse and Lhotse.

If you are searching for **Everest Base Camp trek 2027** options, expect a longer, higher journey than ABC, with rewards to match: Buddhist monasteries, prayer flags, suspension bridges, and big alpine panoramas.

**Why trekkers choose EBC**

- The Everest name: standing at base camp is a genuine bucket-list moment
- Rich Sherpa culture, including monasteries and mountain villages
- Dramatic high-alpine scenery with glaciers and 8,000 m peaks in view
- A well-developed trekking infrastructure, with plenty of teahouses and acclimatization stops

**What to know**

- It takes about two weeks, so it needs more time off and a bigger budget
- Flights to and from Lukla can be delayed by weather, so build in buffer days
- You spend several days above 4,000 m, so acclimatization is essential
- It is cold, especially at night, and the high villages are basic

## Difficulty and altitude sickness: which is harder?

EBC is harder overall, mainly because of altitude and duration, but ABC is physically demanding in its own way.

**Annapurna Base Camp trek difficulty.** ABC peaks at about 4,130 m, which is high enough to feel but below the altitude where most serious problems start. The challenge is the terrain: long, steep staircases up and down through Chhomrong and the lower valley mean tired knees and thighs. Most reasonably fit walkers can finish it with some training.

**Everest Base Camp trek difficulty.** The daily walking is usually gentler than ABC's stairs, but the air gets thin. You spend multiple nights above 4,000 m and reach about 5,364 m at base camp. Altitude, cold and fatigue are the real test, so a slow pace and proper acclimatization days matter more than raw fitness.

**Altitude sickness on both treks**

- Altitude sickness can affect anyone, regardless of fitness or age
- The risk is higher on EBC, but it is not zero on ABC
- Climb slowly, drink plenty of water, and take the planned acclimatization days
- Tell your guide immediately about headaches, nausea, dizziness or poor sleep
- Never ascend with worsening symptoms; descending is the safest treatment
- Choose an itinerary with a gradual ascent profile rather than the cheapest, fastest option

**Training tips**

- Start 8-12 weeks out with hiking, stair climbing and cardio
- Practise with a loaded daypack on hills
- Break in your boots well before you leave

## What most ABC vs EBC guides leave out

Four practical differences decide how the trip actually feels, and they rarely make it into a comparison table.

**1. Where you sleep.** At ABC you sleep at base camp itself (4,130 m) and wake up inside the Annapurna amphitheatre. At EBC you cannot stay overnight at base camp. You sleep in Gorak Shep (about 5,164 m), walk to base camp and return. Base camp is a rocky glacier site, and the classic Everest view comes from Kala Patthar the next morning.

**2. Two kinds of hard.** ABC is hard on your knees and thighs, with thousands of stone steps up and down. EBC is hard on your lungs and sleep, with several nights above 4,000 m and cold nights at the top. Choose the challenge that suits your body.

**3. Flight logistics.** ABC starts with a road transfer from Pokhara, so no flight stands between you and the trailhead. EBC depends on Lukla flights, which are weather-dependent. In peak season they may operate from Manthali (Ramechhap), which means an early-morning drive from Kathmandu. Keep 1-2 spare days before your international flight home. Our 13-day EBC package includes the Kathmandu-Lukla-Kathmandu flights, 2 hotel nights in Kathmandu and breakfast daily, while lunch and dinner on the trail are paid by you at the teahouses, so budget extra for food. Both packages include permits, a government-licensed English-speaking guide, guide and porter insurance, and twin-sharing teahouse accommodation. ## Annapurna Base Camp vs Everest Base Camp scenery, culture and crowds

ABC rewards you with lush variety and a 360-degree mountain amphitheatre; EBC rewards you with raw high-alpine drama and Sherpa culture.

**Scenery.** On ABC you pass through rice terraces, bamboo, moss-draped forest and rhododendrons before emerging into a basin surrounded by peaks. On EBC, the landscape opens up above Namche into a dry, rocky alpine world of glaciers, moraines, and views of Ama Dablam, Lhotse and Everest.

**Culture.** ABC passes Gurung and Magar villages with warm hospitality and local food. EBC takes you through Sherpa country, with Tengboche Monastery, mani walls, prayer flags and a strong Buddhist mountain culture.

**Crowds and teahouses.** Both treks are busy in peak season. EBC has the larger trekking infrastructure, and trails can be crowded in October and April. ABC can be quieter if you start early and avoid the busiest weeks. Teahouses on both routes are simple but welcoming, and rooms get more basic as you climb.

**The honest take:** if you want greenery, variety and a gentler pace, pick ABC. If you want the iconic Everest backdrop and Sherpa heritage, pick EBC.

## Best time to trek ABC and EBC in 2027

Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the best seasons for both treks, with clearer skies and more stable weather.

| Season | ABC | EBC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring (Mar-May) | Excellent; rhododendrons bloom, warm days | Excellent; good views, busy in April and May |
| Monsoon (Jun-Aug) | Wet, leeches, cloud, landslide risk | Cloudy, Lukla flights disrupted, fewer views |
| Autumn (Sep-Nov) | Excellent; the clearest views, the busiest time | Excellent; the most popular window |
| Winter (Dec-Feb) | Cold, snow, avalanche risk near the sanctuary | Very cold; possible, but needs serious gear and flexibility |

**Planning for 2027:** book early for March-May and October-November, because guides, teahouse beds and Lukla flight seats fill up. If you prefer fewer people, aim for the start or end of each season, and keep flexible days in your schedule.

## Which trek should you choose in 2027?

Pick ABC if time, budget or altitude is your main constraint; pick EBC if the Everest experience is the whole point and you have the time.

**Choose Annapurna Base Camp if you...**

- Have 7-10 days
- Want a beginner-friendly first Himalayan trek
- Prefer green landscapes and a gentler altitude profile
- Want to keep the budget down
- Do not want to depend on a Lukla flight
- Are travelling with family or with less experienced trekkers

**Choose Everest Base Camp if you...**

- Have 12-14 days plus buffer days
- Want the bucket-list Everest experience
- Are drawn to Sherpa culture and monasteries
- Are comfortable with cold and high altitude, and will respect acclimatization
- Have a larger budget

**Still undecided?** Ask yourself three questions:

1. How many days can I take, including buffer days?
2. What is my budget, including flights and insurance?
3. Do I care more about the Everest name or about the overall trekking experience?

If you answered "about a week, a moderate budget, and the experience", go for ABC. If you answered "two weeks, a bigger budget, and Everest", go for EBC.

Can't do both in one trip? A common plan is to start with ABC, build confidence at altitude, and return for EBC on a later visit.
