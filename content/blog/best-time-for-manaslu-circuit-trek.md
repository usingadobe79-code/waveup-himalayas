The **Manaslu Circuit Trek best time** depends on two critical factors: stable weather and safe passage over the 5,106m Larkya La Pass. While the **best season for Manaslu Circuit** is widely considered autumn (late September to November) or spring (March to May), the ideal window for you may vary based on whether you prioritize clear mountain views, rhododendron blooms, fewer crowds, or warmer temperatures. This guide from WaveUp Himalayas breaks down the **Manaslu Circuit weather by month**, compares **Manaslu Circuit spring vs autumn**, explores whether you can trek in winter, identifies the **least crowded time**, and pinpoints the **Larkya La Pass best time to cross**—so you can confidently choose the **best time to trek Manaslu Circuit** for your own adventure.

***

## Spring (March to May): Best for Comfort and Flowers

![](/images/blog/manaslu-circuit.jpg "Manaslu Circuit Trek in Spring")

Spring is one of the two best seasons for the **Manaslu Circuit Trek**, and it earns that status on temperature alone. Daytime highs range from roughly **4°C to 20°C**, making spring the more comfortable of the two main trekking windows at lower and mid elevations. If you feel the cold easily or want to enjoy teahouse evenings without layers upon layers, spring is your season.

**March** arrives with cooler overnight temperatures at altitude and occasional lingering winter weather patterns, but daytime conditions are generally stable—and the earliest rhododendron blooms begin appearing along the lower trail. **April** is widely considered the sweet spot for the **Manaslu Circuit Trek**: warmer days, settling weather, and rhododendron forests in full flower around Machha Khola and Namrung. The blooming forests are one of the most photographed stretches of the entire route, and April is when they peak. **May** remains favorable, though pre-monsoon humidity can build slightly and create a touch of haze that marginally softens distant mountain views.

For **Larkya La Pass** conditions, April departures offer the most reliable window of the season. The pass at 5,106m is the defining challenge of the trek, and spring snowpack typically stabilizes by April, giving trekkers the best odds of a safe, clear crossing. If crystal-clear long-range mountain photography is a priority, we still point photographers toward October—but for a balance of comfort, flowers, and reliable pass conditions, April is hard to beat.

There is also a practical advantage to spring that many trekkers overlook: the **Manaslu Restricted Area Permit** costs less outside the autumn peak. In spring, the RAP runs **USD 75 for the first week plus USD 10 for each additional day**, compared to USD 100 plus USD 15 during September to November. For budget-conscious trekkers who still want excellent conditions, spring offers genuinely strong value—and typically thinner crowds than October.

**Spring at a glance:**

- **Best months:** April (sweet spot), March (fewer crowds, early blooms), May (warmer, slight haze)
- **Larkya La Pass:** Most reliable in April
- **Highlights:** Rhododendron blooms, comfortable temperatures, lower permit costs
- **Watch for:** Cooler nights in March, pre-monsoon haze in late May

## Autumn (September to November): Best Views and Stable Weather

![](/images/blog/suspension-bridge-in-manaslu-circuit-trekking-1201.jpg "Manaslu Circuit Trek in Autumn")

Autumn is the season most [**Manaslu Circuit Trek**](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/manaslu-circuit-trek/) trekkers choose, and the reason is atmospheric. The summer monsoon clears out the dust and haze that build up through the dry months, leaving behind the sharpest, clearest mountain views of the entire year. Daytime temperatures range from roughly **6°C to 18°C**—slightly cooler than spring, but with better average visibility for photography and mountain panoramas. If seeing Manaslu, Himalchuli, and Ngadi Chuli at their most defined matters to you, autumn is the season to plan around.

**September** can still carry residual monsoon moisture at lower elevations in the first half of the month, so we generally point trekkers toward **late September onward** rather than the opening week or two. **October** is the peak of the season on this route: the clearest skies of the year, stable temperatures, and the most reliable **Larkya La Pass** conditions of any month. This is the month we book out first, and for good reason. **November** remains stable through most of the month, though overnight temperatures at altitude begin dropping noticeably as the season turns toward winter—early November often delivers nearly October-quality conditions with slightly thinner crowds.

For **Larkya La Pass** conditions, October is the gold standard. The 5,106m crossing is at its most consistent: cold, but typically clear and free of deep snow. The pass is the defining challenge of the **Manaslu Circuit Trek**, and autumn gives you the best odds of a safe, straightforward crossing without the snowpack uncertainty that comes with early spring or late autumn.

The trade-off for autumn's superb conditions is cost and demand. The **Manaslu Restricted Area Permit** costs **USD 100 for the first seven days** during September to November, plus USD 15 for each additional day—higher than the spring rate. Popular October departure dates with reputable operators sell out months in advance, and teahouses along the route fill up faster. If you have flexibility, late September or early November often delivers excellent conditions with slightly more breathing room.

**Autumn at a glance:**

- **Best months:** October (peak), late September (fewer crowds), early November (thinner crowds, colder nights)
- **Larkya La Pass:** Most reliable in October
- **Highlights:** Clearest mountain views of the year, stable weather, superb photography conditions
- **Watch for:** Higher permit costs, busier teahouses, early-season monsoon residue in September, dropping temperatures in late November

## Winter (December to February): Risks and Larkya La Closures

![](/images/blog/manaslu-circuit-trek-route-1778046052.jpg "Manaslu Circuit Trek in Winter")

Winter on the **Manaslu Circuit Trek** is not a season to take lightly. This is the one window where the route's defining challenge—the **Larkya La Pass** at 5,106m—can become genuinely impassable. Snowfall can close the pass entirely, and unlike lower-altitude treks where a closed pass simply means a detour, on the Manaslu Circuit it can strand an itinerary partway through the loop with no way to complete the route as planned. For most trekkers, this is the season to avoid.

The cold is the first obstacle. At altitude, temperatures stay **well below freezing**, and the high camps around Samdo and Dharmasala regularly drop to **-20°C or lower**. January is the harshest month on the entire circuit, with the pass often impassable under deep snow and most teahouses above Namrung closed. February offers a slight improvement and occasionally aligns with **Lhosar**, the local New Year, but the snowpack risk remains high through the month.

**Early December** is the one window within winter that experienced cold-weather trekkers sometimes target. The trail is often still dry, and the pass can remain passable with solid winter gear—but conditions deteriorate noticeably as the month progresses, and by late December you are approaching January-level caution. Even in early December, the margin for error is narrow.

Teahouse availability is the second major constraint. High-altitude villages including **Samdo and Dharmasala** see owners move down to lower elevations to escape the cold, and teahouses at **Dharmasala and Bimthang shut down completely** in winter. The handful of operators who run winter departures do so with **professionally organized tent camps** and expedition-grade equipment, because the normal teahouse infrastructure simply is not there.

What winter does offer is solitude and clarity. The trails are nearly empty, the skies are often the clearest of the year, and the snow-covered landscapes are spectacular. But this is a season for trekkers with genuine winter mountaineering experience, flexible itineraries, and proper expedition-grade gear—not for a first **Manaslu Circuit Trek**.

**Winter at a glance:**

- **Best window (relatively):** Early December, before snow risk escalates
- **Larkya La Pass:** High risk of closure; January–February often impassable
- **Highlights:** Deep solitude, clear winter skies, snow-covered scenery, Lhosar in February
- **Watch for:** Extreme cold (-20°C or lower at high camps), teahouse closures at Samdo, Dharmasala, and Bimthang, avalanche risk, short daylight hours

## Monsoon (June to August): Avoid for Most Trekkers

![](/images/blog/Manaslu-Circuit-Trek-in-Autumn.SEP_.webp "Manaslu Circuit Trek in Monsoon")

The monsoon is the one season we actively steer **Manaslu Circuit Trek** trekkers away from. Between June and August, the trail becomes genuinely difficult and at times unsafe, and the experience you came for—the views, the pass, the mountain scenery—is largely unavailable. For all but the most seasoned Nepal trekkers with specific research objectives, this is a season to skip.

The problems compound quickly. Heavy rain turns lower-elevation trails into mud, and the sections through the Budhi Gandaki valley become slippery and slow. **Leeches** are a persistent nuisance at lower altitudes, particularly in the forested stretches before Namrung. More seriously, the monsoon brings **landslide risk** on road sections and steep trail cuts, and stream crossings can swell unpredictably. Mountain visibility is poor for much of the season, with Manaslu and the surrounding peaks often hidden behind cloud for days at a time.

The **Larkya La Pass** is rarely the main obstacle in monsoon—the bigger issue is simply getting to it. The pass itself is often snow-free in summer, but the approach is wet, exposed, and prone to washed-out trail sections. Fog and rain reduce visibility on the pass crossing itself, which is exactly the wrong combination on a 5,106m exposed ridge.

**June** is the transition month, with the monsoon arriving in earnest around mid-month. **July and August** are the wettest, and **late August into early September** often brings the tail end of the monsoon with lingering moisture. Teahouses remain open through the season, and prices are lower, but the trade-off is a trek that is wet, muddy, and frequently viewless.

**Monsoon at a glance:**

- **Best window (relatively):** Late August into early September, as the monsoon begins to retreat
- **Larkya La Pass:** Approach trails wet and exposed; poor visibility on the crossing
- **Highlights:** Empty trails, low prices, lush green valleys
- **Watch for:** Heavy rain, leeches, landslide risk, swollen streams, poor mountain visibility, muddy trails

## Manaslu Circuit Spring vs Autumn: Which Season Is Right for You?

Both spring and autumn are excellent windows for the **Manaslu Circuit Trek best time**, but they offer different strengths. This **Manaslu Circuit spring vs autumn** comparison will help you decide based on what matters most to you.

### Side-by-Side Comparison

| Factor | Spring (March–May) | Autumn (September–November) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Best Month** | April | October |
| **Daytime Temperature** | 4°C to 20°C (warmer) | 6°C to 18°C (slightly cooler) |
| **Mountain Visibility** | Good, but hazy by late May | Clearest of the year |
| **Trail Conditions** | Stable; lingering snow in early March | Dry and firm; best pass conditions |
| **Larkya La Pass** | Most reliable in April | Most reliable in October |
| **Scenery Highlights** | Rhododendron blooms, lush forests | Sharp mountain panoramas, crisp air |
| **Crowds** | Moderate; March and early May quieter | High in October; late September/November quieter |
| **Permit Cost (RAP)** | USD 75 first week + USD 10/day | USD 100 first week + USD 15/day |
| **Best For** | Comfort, flowers, budget | Clear views, photography, stability |

### Choose Spring If You Want:

- **Warmer daytime temperatures** and more comfortable teahouse evenings
- **Rhododendron forests in full bloom**—April is the peak
- **Lower permit costs** and slightly thinner crowds than October
- **Reliable Larkya La Pass conditions** in April

### Choose Autumn If You Want:

- **The clearest mountain views of the year**—ideal for photography
- **The most stable weather** and driest trails
- **The most reliable Larkya La Pass crossing** in October
- **Peak-season energy** on the trail (if you enjoy meeting other trekkers)

There is no wrong choice between spring and autumn—only the season that matches your priorities. If you value **comfort and flowers**, choose **April**. If you value **clarity and stability**, choose **October**. And if you want the **Manaslu Circuit least crowded time** with still-good conditions, aim for **late September**, **late November**, or **March**.

## When Is the Least Crowded Time on the Manaslu Circuit?

The least crowded time on the Manaslu Circuit falls in the **shoulder seasons that bookend the two popular trekking windows**, rather than during the peak months themselves. October and April are the busiest periods, but the weeks just before or after them offer a remarkably quieter experience without sacrificing good trekking conditions.

### The Quietest Windows: Late September & Late November

For trekkers who want to combine solid weather with genuine solitude, the best choices are **late September** and **late November**. During these windows, you can expect **60–70% fewer trekkers** than the October peak, while still benefiting from generally excellent weather and trail conditions.

- **Late September:** The monsoon retreats, leaving the landscape intensely green and fresh, but the main October crowds have not yet arrived. Teahouses above Namrung feel significantly quieter, and the trail retains its remote character.
- **Late November:** The post-monsoon clarity often lingers into late November, but the trail traffic drops sharply. The trade-off is colder mornings and nights, and snow becomes possible on the Larkya La later in the month, so flexibility is advisable.

### The Quietest Spring Options: March & Early May

If you prefer a spring trek, **March** and **early May** are your best bets for fewer people.

- **March** sees the **lowest crowd numbers of the spring season**. It is a favorite for trekkers seeking a quieter trail, with stable conditions and fewer organized groups on the route. Early March can still carry lingering winter snow at the pass, so a flexible itinerary helps.
- **Early May** also offers a peaceful experience before the pre-monsoon clouds and humidity build later in the month. Crowds remain moderate, and the rhododendron blooms at lower elevations are still a highlight.

### The Absolute Quietest (With Caveats): Winter

**January** is technically the quietest month of all, with **very low crowds** and crisp, clear skies. However, this comes with significant trade-offs: extreme cold, a high risk of the Larkya La Pass being closed by snow, and many high-altitude teahouses shutting down for the season. It is only a realistic option for experienced winter trekkers with full cold-weather gear and a guided, flexible plan.

### A Note on What "Quiet" Means on Manaslu

Even during peak season, the Manaslu Circuit never reaches the crowd levels of Everest or Annapurna, thanks to its restricted-area status and limited infrastructure. Choosing a shoulder-season window simply enhances that natural sense of remoteness, giving you a better chance of walking for hours without meeting other groups and enjoying calmer evenings in the teahouses.
