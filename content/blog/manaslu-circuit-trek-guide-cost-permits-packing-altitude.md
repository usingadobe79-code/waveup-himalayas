**Total Manaslu Permit Cost**

The total **Manaslu Circuit permit cost** for a standard trek typically ranges from **USD 150 to USD 265**, depending on the season and trek length.

**Manaslu Restricted Area Permit (RAP) Rules**

- **Solo trekking is not permitted** on the Manaslu Circuit.
- You must trek with a **licensed guide** and obtain permits through a **registered trekking agency**.
- **Minimum group size:** 2 trekkers (though some agencies can arrange solo treks with a guide).
- Permits are checked at multiple checkpoints along the route.

**Do You Need a Guide for Manaslu Circuit?**

**Yes.** The **Manaslu Circuit** is a **restricted area trek**, and **TIMS (Trekkers' Information Management System)** cards are not issued for this route. You must have a **licensed guide** and all necessary permits arranged through a **registered trekking agency** like **WaveUp Himalayas**.

**Important:** Attempting the **Manaslu Circuit without a guide** is illegal and can result in fines, permit confiscation, or deportation.

## Manaslu Circuit Itinerary: Day-by-Day Breakdown

The [**Manaslu Circuit Trek**](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/manaslu-circuit-trek/) follows a carefully designed route that allows for proper acclimatization while showcasing the very best of the **Manaslu region**. Below is a detailed **14-day Manaslu Circuit itinerary** that balances challenging trekking days with rest and exploration. Every itinerary is fully customizable—dates, pace, and group size can be tailored to your preferences.

### Day-by-Day Details

**Day 1: Drive from Kathmandu to Machhakhola (870m) – 8–9 hrs**
A scenic drive through hills, rivers, and villages. Highlights include the Trishuli River and off-road adventure.

**Day 2: Trek from Machhakhola to Jagat (1,340m) – 6–7 hrs**
Follow the Budhi Gandaki River on a gradual ascent. Highlights include suspension bridges, hot springs at Tatopani, and waterfalls.

**Day 3: Trek from Jagat to Deng (1,860m) – 6–7 hrs**
Enter the restricted **Manaslu region**. Highlights include Tibetan-influenced culture, mani walls, and gorges.

**Day 4: Trek from Deng to Namrung (2,630m) – 6–7 hrs**
A gradual climb with better views. Highlights include the first Manaslu-region peaks and pine forests.

**Day 5: Trek from Namrung to Samagaon (3,530m) – 6–7 hrs**
Enter the high Himalayan zone and Tibetan settlements. Highlights include **Mount Manaslu** views, monasteries, and yak pastures.

**Day 6: Acclimatization Day in Samagaon (3,530m)**
Rest and explore for adaptation. Highlights include **Manaslu Base Camp** or Pungyen Gompa, and glaciers.

**Day 7: Trek from Samagaon to Samdo (3,860m) – 4–5 hrs**
A short trek with gradual gain. Highlights include Tibetan culture and views toward Tibet.

**Day 8: Acclimatization Day in Samdo (3,860m)**
Rest and optional hike. Highlights include a Tibet viewpoint and glacier panoramas.

**Day 9: Trek from Samdo to Dharamsala (4,460m) – 4–5 hrs**
Trek to the base of **Larke Pass**. Highlights include alpine terrain and high-pass preparation.

**Day 10: Trek from Dharamsala to Larke Pass to Bimthang (5,160m) – 8–9 hrs**
A long, challenging pass day. Highlights include the **Larke Pass** crossing, panoramas, and glaciers.

**Day 11: Trek from Bimthang to Tilije (2,300m) – 6–7 hrs**
Descend through forests and valleys. Highlights include Himlung and Annapurna views, and rhododendrons.

**Day 12: Trek from Tilije to Dharapani (1,960m) – 5–6 hrs**
Join the **Annapurna Circuit** trail. Highlights include mixed-culture villages and greenery.

**Day 13: Drive from Dharapani to Besisahar to Kathmandu (1,400m) – 8–9 hrs**
Scenic drive to the capital. Highlights include the Marsyangdi valley and terraced farms.

**Day 14: Departure Day**
Transfer to the airport. Trip completion.

## Best Time to Trek the Manaslu Circuit

Choosing the right season is one of the most important decisions when planning your **Manaslu Circuit Trek**. The weather, temperature, trail conditions, and mountain views vary dramatically throughout the year. This[ **best time to trek Manaslu Circuit**](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/best-time-for-manaslu-circuit-trek/) guide breaks down each season so you can choose the ideal window for your **Manaslu Circuit Trek 2026**.

### **Manaslu Circuit in Autumn (September–November)**

Autumn is the **best time to trek the Manaslu Circuit** for most trekkers. The monsoon clears out, leaving crisp, clean air and spectacular mountain views.

**Pros:**

- Clear skies and excellent visibility
- Stable weather with minimal rain
- Best views of **Mount Manaslu**, **Himalchuli**, and **Ganesh Himal**
- Festivals like Dashain and Tihar add cultural color

**Cons:**

- Peak season means more trekkers
- Higher **permit fees** (September–November)
- Teahouses can be busy

**Temperature:**

- Daytime: 10–20°C
- Nighttime: -5 to 5°C
- Larkya La Pass: Can drop to -10°C or lower

**Verdict:** **Autumn is the best season** for clear views, stable weather, and an unforgettable **Manaslu Circuit Trek** experience.

### **Manaslu Circuit in Spring (March–May)**

Spring is the second-best season for the **Manaslu Circuit Trek**. The trails are quieter than autumn, and the rhododendron forests are in full bloom.

**Pros:**

- Mild temperatures at lower elevations
- Beautiful blooming rhododendrons and wildflowers
- Fewer crowds than autumn
- Good mountain views, especially in March and April

**Cons:**

- Afternoon clouds can obscure peaks
- Occasional pre-monsoon rain in May
- Snow may still linger on **Larkya La Pass** in early March

**Temperature:**

- Daytime: 12–22°C
- Nighttime: -3 to 8°C
- Larkya La Pass: -5 to -10°C

**Verdict:** **Spring is an excellent choice** for trekkers who want fewer crowds, blooming forests, and comfortable temperatures.

### **Manaslu Circuit in Winter (December–February)**

Winter trekking on the **Manaslu Circuit** is possible but challenging. Cold temperatures and snow-blocked passes make this season suitable only for experienced trekkers.

**Pros:**

- Very few trekkers — complete solitude
- Lower **permit fees** (off-season rates)
- Clear skies on good days

**Cons:**

- Extreme cold, especially at altitude
- **Larkya La Pass** may be impassable due to snow
- Some teahouses close for the season
- Shorter daylight hours

**Temperature:**

- Daytime: 5–12°C
- Nighttime: -15 to -5°C
- Larkya La Pass: -20°C or lower

**Verdict:** **Not recommended** for most trekkers. Only attempt if you have extensive winter trekking experience and proper gear.

### **Manaslu Circuit in Monsoon (June–August)**

The monsoon season brings heavy rain, landslides, and poor visibility. The **Manaslu Circuit** is generally not recommended during this period.

**Pros:**

- Lush green landscapes
- Very few trekkers
- Lower accommodation costs

**Cons:**

- Heavy rain and slippery trails
- Landslides and washed-out roads
- Leeches in lower elevations
- Poor mountain views
- High risk of flight delays

**Temperature:**

- Daytime: 15–25°C
- Nighttime: 5–15°C

**Verdict:** **Avoid the monsoon** unless you are prepared for wet, muddy, and potentially dangerous conditions.

## Manaslu Circuit Altitude and Safety

Altitude sickness is a serious concern on the **Manaslu Circuit**, especially as you approach **Larkya La Pass (5,106m)** . Here's how to stay safe.

![](/images/blog/71d324db-8436-4e18-806b-2144be3880e5.png)

**Tips to Prevent Altitude Sickness**

1. **Ascend Gradually:** Follow a slow, steady ascent. Our **Manaslu Circuit itinerary** includes an acclimatization day in Samagaun.
2. **Stay Hydrated:** Drink at least 3–4 liters of water per day.
3. **Avoid Alcohol:** Alcohol worsens altitude symptoms.
4. **Listen to Your Body:** Headaches, nausea, and dizziness are early signs of **AMS (Acute Mountain Sickness)** .
5. **Carry Diamox:** Consult your doctor about **Diamox** as a preventive measure.
6. **Descend if Symptoms Worsen:** The only real treatment for severe altitude sickness is **immediate descent**.

**Manaslu Circuit Travel Insurance**

Ensure your **travel insurance** covers **high-altitude trekking** and **emergency helicopter evacuation** above 5,000m. At **WaveUp Himalayas**, we can recommend reputable insurance providers.

## Manaslu Circuit Accommodation and Food

### Teahouse Accommodation

On the [**Manaslu Circuit Trek**](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/manaslu-circuit-trek/), teahouse accommodation and local food are simple, authentic, and part of the adventure. Here are the key highlights to know before you go.

![](/images/blog/tea-house-accommodation-manaslu-circuit-1-1024x683.webp "Tea Houses Manaslu Circuit")

**Accommodation Highlights**

- **Teahouse lodging** – Family-run lodges with twin-share rooms, basic beds, and blankets.
- **Bathrooms** – Attached at lower elevations; shared squat toilets higher up.
- **Hot showers** – Available for a fee (NPR 200–500) at lower altitudes; bucket or none above Samagaon.
- **Electricity** – Charging costs NPR 100–300 per hour; solar power is limited at altitude.
- **Wifi** – Rare and unreliable above Namrung; expect to be offline.
- **Heating** – Dining hall stove only; bedrooms are unheated.
- **Sleeping bag** – Bring a 4-season bag rated -10°C to -15°C; blankets alone aren't enough.
- **Cost** – NPR 300–2,000 per night; often discounted if you eat at the same teahouse.
- **Essentials** – Carry cash, power bank, toilet paper, earplugs, and a sleeping bag liner.

**Food Highlights**

- **Dal Bhat** – The staple meal: rice, lentil soup, vegetable curry, pickles, with unlimited refills.
- **Tibetan dishes** – Thukpa (noodle soup), momo (dumplings), Tibetan bread, tsampa.
- **Other options** – Fried noodles, fried rice, eggs, pancakes, porridge, potatoes.
- **Vegetarian** – Easy to find; vegan and gluten-free options possible with care.
- **Meat** – Avoid above 3,000m for freshness and safety.
- **Water** – Boiled water safe (NPR 100–300/L); bring purification tablets; avoid bottled water.
- **Budget** – NPR 1,900–3,100 (USD 20–30) per day for meals and drinks.
- **Prices rise** – Higher altitude means higher costs, especially at Dharamsala (4,460m).
- **Etiquette** – Eat where you sleep; teahouses rely on meal income.

Teahouse accommodation and food on the **Manaslu Circuit Trek** are basic but cozy, nutritious, and culturally rich. With the right preparation and an adventurous palate, you'll stay comfortable and well-fueled for the journey ahead.

## Manaslu Circuit Connectivity and Money

**Introduction:** Staying connected and managing money on the **Manaslu Circuit Trek** requires preparation. This remote **restricted area trek** has limited internet, unreliable mobile signals, and no ATMs. Here's what to expect and how to plan.

**Connectivity Highlights**

- **Wifi** – Available at some teahouses for a fee (NPR 200–500), but slow and unreliable, especially above **Namrung**.
- **Mobile network** – Ncell and NTC have patchy coverage in lower villages; signal disappears above **Samagaon**.
- **SIM cards** – Buy an Ncell or NTC SIM in Kathmandu before the trek. Carry your passport for registration.
- **Charging** – Solar power is limited. Charging devices costs NPR 100–300 per hour at higher altitudes. Carry a **power bank**.
- **Offline period** – Expect to be offline for much of the trek, especially from **Samdo to Bimthang**.
- **Emergency communication** – Guides carry phones or satellite devices for emergencies. Don't rely on personal connectivity.

**Money Highlights**

- **ATMs** – None on the **Manaslu Circuit**. Withdraw all cash in Kathmandu or Besisahar.
- **Currency** – Nepalese Rupees (NPR). Carry small denominations for ease.
- **Cash needed** – Budget NPR 30,000–50,000 for meals, snacks, drinks, hot showers, charging, and tips.
- **Card payments** – Not accepted anywhere on the trek.
- **Costs** – Food and lodging: NPR 1,900–3,100 per day. Hot showers: NPR 200–500. Charging: NPR 100–300/hour.
- **Tips** – Budget NPR 1,000–2,000 per day for guide and porter tips (optional but appreciated).
- **Extra cash** – Carry a little more than planned for emergencies or unexpected expenses.
- **Safe storage** – Keep cash in a money belt or hidden pouch. Don't flash large amounts.

Connectivity and money on the **Manaslu Circuit Trek** are limited by design—this is a remote, restricted region. Carry enough cash, buy a local SIM in Kathmandu, bring a power bank, and embrace the digital detox. With proper preparation, you'll stay comfortable and focused on the adventure.
