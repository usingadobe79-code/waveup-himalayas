**Mount Everest is located in the Himalayas, on the border between Nepal and the Tibet Autonomous Region of China, and its summit stands 8,848.86 meters (29,031.7 feet) above sea level.** It is the highest mountain on Earth, and every year thousands of trekkers, climbers and curious travelers ask the same first question: **where exactly is Mount Everest?**

At WaveUp Himalayas, we answer that question for travelers every day, because we live and work in the shadow of these mountains. Whether you are planning an Everest Base Camp trek, comparing the Nepal and Tibet sides, or simply looking at a world map and wondering where the world's tallest peak sits, this post gives you everything you need in one place.

## Mount Everest Location at a Glance

Mount Everest sits on the Nepal–China border in the Mahalangur Himal, a sub-range of the Himalayas in Asia.

| Detail | Mount Everest |
| --- | --- |
| Continent | Asia |
| Countries | Nepal and China (Tibet Autonomous Region) |
| Mountain range | Himalayas, Mahalangur Himal sub-range |
| Coordinates | About 27.99° N, 86.93° E |

| Protected area (Nepal) | Sagarmatha National Park |

## Which Country Is Mount Everest In? Nepal or Tibet?

Mount Everest is in both [Nepa](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/where-is-nepal-located/)l and China. The international border runs across the summit ridge, so the south side of the mountain belongs to Nepal and the north side belongs to the Tibet Autonomous Region of China.

This is why you will see two different answers online. Some people say "Mount Everest is in Nepal" because the most popular trekking and climbing routes start there. Others say "Mount Everest is in Tibet" because the north face and the northern base camp are in China. Both are correct, because the peak is shared.

- **Nepal side:** The southern slopes lie in the Solukhumbu district of eastern Nepal, inside Sagarmatha National Park.
- **Tibet side:** The northern slopes lie in Tingri County of the Shigatse region, in the Tibet Autonomous Region of China.

For most international trekkers, the Nepal side is the easier and more scenic way to reach the mountain, because it offers well-established trails, Sherpa villages, teahouses and the famous Everest Base Camp trek.

## Exact Location of Mount Everest: Coordinates, Range and Map

The GPS coordinates of Mount Everest are approximately 27°59′ N latitude and 86°55′ E longitude, or about 27.99° N, 86.93° E in decimal degrees. If you type these numbers into any online map, the pin lands on the summit.

![Exact Location of Mount Everest](/images/blog/mt-everest-map-by-freeworldmaps-002.jpg "Exact Location of Mount Everest")

**What continent is Mount Everest in?** Asia. It rises in the central-eastern part of the Himalayan arc, which stretches across Pakistan, India, Nepal, Bhutan and China.

**What mountain range is Mount Everest in?** The Himalayas, within the Mahalangur Himal sub-range. Neighboring giants include Lhotse (8,516 m), Makalu (8,485 m) and Cho Oyu (8,188 m), and Nuptse sits right beside Everest.

**Where is Mount Everest on a world map?** Look at the northern edge of the Indian subcontinent. Nepal sits between India to the south and China's Tibet to the north, and Everest is in the northeast of Nepal, roughly 140 km (about 87 miles) in a straight line from Kathmandu.

**Which national park protects it?** On the Nepal side, Everest sits inside [Sagarmatha National Park](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/sagarmatha-national-park/), a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979. The park covers glaciers, high alpine valleys and Sherpa communities such as Namche Bazaar, Khumjung and Tengboche.

## Sagarmatha, Chomolungma and Everest: One Mountain, Three Names

Mount Everest goes by different names depending on who you ask and which side you stand on.

![Sagarmatha, Chomolungma and Everest](/images/blog/1596997438-001.jpg "Sagarmatha, Chomolungma and Everest")

- **Sagarmatha:** The Nepali name, usually translated as "forehead of the sky." It was officially adopted in Nepal in the 1960s, and the national park carries the same name.
- **Chomolungma (Qomolangma):** The Tibetan name, often translated as "Holy Mother" or "Goddess Mother of the World."
- **Mount Everest:** The English name, given in 1865 in honor of Sir George Everest, a former Surveyor General of India. Before that, British surveyors had recorded the mountain simply as Peak XV.

For the Sherpa people who live in the Khumbu valley, the mountain has long been sacred, and many local families still hold a ceremony called a puja before an expedition. Our WaveUp Himalayas guides will happily tell you more about these traditions when you walk the trail with us.

## How Tall Is Mount Everest?

Mount Everest is 8,848.86 meters (29,031.7 feet) tall, measured from sea level to the snow summit. Nepal and China announced this figure together in December 2020, after separate surveys by each country and a joint agreement on the result.

Earlier figures differed slightly. For decades, 8,848 m was the number most people used, and Chinese surveys had at times reported a lower rock-height figure. The 2020 joint announcement settled the question for official purposes.

Everest is the highest mountain _above sea level_. Other peaks are taller if measured from base to summit, but none rises higher into the sky than Everest.

## Everest Base Camp: Nepal Side vs Tibet Side

Because the mountain is shared, there are two Everest Base Camps. They differ in access, scenery and style of travel.

|  | Nepal side (South) | Tibet side (North) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base camp | Everest Base Camp, about 5,364 m | North Base Camp, about 5,150 m |
| Region | Khumbu, Solukhumbu district | Tingri County, Tibet Autonomous Region |
| Climbing route | South Col route | North Ridge route |
| How you get there | Fly Kathmandu to Lukla, then trek | Travel overland from Lhasa or Kathmandu with permits |
| Style | Multi-day trek through Sherpa villages | Road access to the base camp area |
| Best for | Trekkers, photographers, first-time Himalayan travelers | Travelers combining Tibet culture with an Everest view |

Most of our guests choose the Nepal side. The trail passes through Namche Bazaar, Tengboche Monastery and Dingboche, with views of Ama Dablam, Lhotse and Nuptse, and it gives you time to adjust to the altitude gradually.

## How to Get to Mount Everest from Kathmandu

Everything starts in Kathmandu, Nepal's capital and the arrival point for international flights at Tribhuvan International Airport. From there, the journey to the Everest region follows a clear path.

![Lukla Airport, officially Tenzing–Hillary Airport](/images/blog/lukla%20airport%20landing-001-1.jpg "Lukla Airport, officially Tenzing–Hillary Airport")

1. **Arrive in Kathmandu.** Rest, sort your permits and meet your guide.
2. **Fly to Lukla.** The short mountain [flight to Lukla](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/flight-to-lukla-safety-delays-what-to-expect/) (about 2,860 m), the gateway to the Khumbu region, is one of the most memorable parts of the trip. During busy seasons, departures may operate from Ramechhap instead of Kathmandu, and your trip team will plan for that.
3. **Trek to Everest Base Camp.** From Lukla you follow the Dudh Koshi valley north through Phakding, Namche Bazaar, Tengboche, Dingboche and Lobuche to Gorak Shep, then on to Everest Base Camp.
4. **Climb Kala Patthar (optional).** At about 5,545 m, it is the best viewpoint for Mount Everest itself, since the peak is partly hidden from Base Camp.

The summit is about 140 km from Kathmandu in a straight line, but there are no roads across the mountains in between, so the real travel time is measured in days of walking, not hours of driving.

**Permits you will need on the Nepal side:** a Sagarmatha National Park entry permit and a local Khumbu municipality permit. Your trekking company arranges these as part of the trip, and requirements can change, so always confirm the latest rules before you travel.

## Best Time to Visit Mount Everest

The two [best trekking seasons for Everest Base Camp](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/best-time-for-everest-base-camp-trek/) are spring and autumn, when skies are clearer and conditions are more stable.

| Season | Months | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring | March to May | Rhododendrons in bloom, warmer days, and the main Everest climbing window in May |
| Monsoon | June to August | Rain, cloud and leeches at lower altitudes, with limited mountain views |
| Autumn | September to November | Crisp air, stable weather and some of the clearest Himalayan views of the year |
| Winter | December to February | Very cold, especially at night, but quiet trails and sharp views |

Most summit expeditions climb in the short pre-monsoon window in May, when winds ease high on the mountain. Trekkers can choose either spring or autumn, and both seasons offer excellent views. Book early for either season, since lodges and flights fill up quickly.
