If you're planning the Manaslu Circuit Trek, the permit system is the first thing to understand—because it works differently from almost every other trek in Nepal. You cannot simply show up in Kathmandu, buy a permit, and start walking. The **Manaslu Circuit Trek permits** are tied to a restricted-area system that requires a registered Nepali agency and a licensed guide before you even leave the city.

This guide breaks down exactly which permits you need, what they cost in 2026, how the application process works, and the mistakes that can derail your trek before it begins.

***

## Why Manaslu Permits Are Different From Other Nepal Treks

The Manaslu region was closed to foreign trekkers until 1991, and it remains a controlled area today. Unlike Everest Base Camp or the Annapurna Circuit, where you can arrange permits independently and trek without a guide, Manaslu's Restricted Area Permit is only issued through a registered trekking agency, with a licensed guide named on the paperwork .

This isn't a formality. Checkpoints at Jagat, Namrung, Sama Gaon, and Dharapani verify your permits and guide credentials against the agency's booking. Trekkers without proper documentation are turned back—no exceptions, no permits sold on the trail .

The practical takeaway: your Manaslu trek starts with choosing the right agency, not with a permit counter at the Nepal Tourism Board.

***

## The Three Permits You Need for the Manaslu Circuit

Every foreign trekker on the Manaslu Circuit needs three separate permits. They serve different purposes and are checked at different points along the route.

### 1. Manaslu Restricted Area Permit (RAP)

This is the core permit and the reason the Manaslu Circuit operates under tighter rules than other Nepal treks. The RAP governs access to the restricted zone around Mount Manaslu and is priced by season, not by trekker nationality.

In peak season (September to November), the RAP costs USD 100 for the first seven days, then USD 15 for each additional day. Outside that window—December through August—the fee drops to USD 75 for the first week, then USD 10 per extra day .

Most standard Manaslu itineraries run 12 to 14 days, so you'll pay for the initial week plus five to seven extra days regardless of season.

### 2. Manaslu Conservation Area Permit (MCAP)

The MCAP supports conservation and infrastructure in the Manaslu region. It costs NPR 3,000, roughly USD 23 to 25 at current exchange rates, and is checked at Jagat as you enter the conservation area .

Your agency typically purchases this alongside the RAP. You don't need to visit a separate office.

### 3. Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP)

Here's the detail many trekkers miss: the Manaslu Circuit doesn't end in Manaslu territory. After crossing Larkya La Pass, the route descends into the Annapurna Conservation Area near Dharapani. That means you need an ACAP as well—another NPR 3,000, or about USD 23 to 25 .

The ACAP is checked at Dharapani on your way out. Without it, you cannot legally exit through that checkpoint.

## The Guide Rule: You Cannot Apply for Manaslu Permits Yourself

This is the single most important thing to understand about Manaslu Circuit Trek permits: **there is no independent application process**. The Restricted Area Permit is issued only against a registered trekking agency's booking, with a licensed guide attached to your group before the trek begins .

Some trekkers ask whether they can hire a freelance guide and arrange permits separately. The answer is no—the agency must be registered with the Trekking Agencies Association of Nepal (TAAN) or the Department of Immigration, and the guide's license must be verified through that agency's paperwork .

## Can Solo Trekkers Get Manaslu Permits in 2026?

Yes—with one condition. As of March 2026, the previous rule requiring a minimum of two trekkers has been removed, meaning solo travelers can now obtain permits individually .

However, the guide requirement remains unchanged. A solo trekker still needs a licensed guide through a registered agency. You'll pay the same permit fees as anyone else, but the guide cost will be yours alone unless you join a group departure where costs are shared.

For solo trekkers on a budget, joining a fixed group departure is often the most practical option—you share guide and transport costs while still getting the full Manaslu experience.

## Where Your Permits Are Checked on the Trail

Your Manaslu Circuit Trek permits are verified at four main checkpoints along the route. Knowing where they sit—and what each one inspects—helps you understand why every document must be correct before you start walking.

![](/images/blog/manaslu-permit-checkpost.png "Manaslu Circuit Trek Permit Checkpoint")

| Checkpoint | Altitude | Permits Checked | What Happens Here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Jagat** | 1,410m | RAP entry, MCAP | First inspection as you enter the restricted and conservation area. A local municipal fee (around NPR 1,000) may also be collected here. |
| **Namrung** | 2,630m | RAP, MCAP | Mid-route verification. Officials match your passport against your permit details and confirm your guide's credentials. |
| **Sama Gaon** | 3,530m | MCAP, occasional police checks | The largest settlement on the trek and a common acclimatisation stop. Police may review your documents here independently of the conservation office. |
| **Dharapani** | 1,963m | RAP exit, ACAP | Final checkpoint after Larkya La. Your RAP is logged as exited and your ACAP is verified as you leave the Manaslu restricted zone and enter Annapurna territory. |

### What Officials Actually Check

At each checkpoint, three things are verified:

- **Your passport** against the name and number printed on your permit
- **Your permit dates** to confirm you're trekking within the authorised window
- **Your guide's licence and agency registration**, matched to the booking that generated your permits

If any of these don't align—expired dates, a missing permit, an unregistered operator—you will not be allowed to continue. There is no permit office on the trail and no way to resolve documentation issues once you've left Kathmandu.

## Documents You Need for Manaslu Permit Applications

Your agency will request these before processing your permits:

- **Valid passport** (original required for final issuance; copies for advance processing)
- **Passport-sized photographs** (typically two to four copies)
- **Personal details** (name, nationality, passport number, date of birth)
- **Travel insurance details** (increasingly requested, though not universally required for the permit itself)

Submit these to your agency at least six to eight weeks before departure, especially for autumn trekking when permit processing queues lengthen.

***

## Common Manaslu Permit Mistakes to Avoid

**Assuming you can arrange permits in Kathmandu on arrival.** You can't. The RAP requires agency processing before your trek starts.

**Booking with an operator who isn't properly registered.** Unusually low prices sometimes signal an agency cutting corners on permits or guide credentials. Check TAAN registration before paying a deposit.

**Not confirming what's included in your package.** Ask explicitly: "Does this price include the RAP, MCAP, and ACAP?" Get the answer in writing .

**Planning too close to your departure date.** Peak-season permits and guide availability tighten months in advance. October and April departures are especially competitive now that Manaslu trekker numbers have grown significantly.
