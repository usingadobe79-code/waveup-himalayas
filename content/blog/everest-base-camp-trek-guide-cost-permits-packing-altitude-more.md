## Everest Base Camp Trek Route

The [**Everest Base Camp Trek** ](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/everest-base-camp-trek/)**route** takes you through Nepal’s beautiful Khumbu region, passing Sherpa villages, Buddhist monasteries, suspension bridges and spectacular Himalayan landscapes. The classic trek starts with a flight from Kathmandu to **Lukla** and gradually climbs toward Everest Base Camp. At **WaveUp Himalayas**, we include proper acclimatization days to help trekkers adjust to the increasing altitude.

**Kathmandu to Lukla**

The journey begins in Kathmandu with a flight to **Lukla**, the main gateway to the Everest region. From Lukla, the trek begins toward Phakding.

**Lukla to Namche Bazaar**

The trail follows the Dudh Koshi River through forests and small villages before reaching **Namche Bazaar**, an important Sherpa town and acclimatization stop.

**Namche Bazaar to Tengboche**

From Namche, the trail continues to **Tengboche**, famous for its Buddhist monastery and beautiful views of Everest, Ama Dablam, Lhotse and Nuptse.

**Tengboche to Dingboche**

The route continues toward **Dingboche**, where trekkers usually take an acclimatization day before heading higher into the mountains.

**Dingboche to Lobuche and Gorak Shep**

The landscape becomes more rugged as you climb toward **Lobuche** and then **Gorak Shep**. The increasing altitude makes walking slower and more challenging.

**Gorak Shep to Everest Base Camp**

From Gorak Shep, you trek alongside the Khumbu Glacier toward **Everest Base Camp**, located at approximately **5,364 metres**. Reaching base camp is one of the main highlights of the journey.

**Kala Patthar and Return**

Many trekkers hike to **Kala Patthar** for spectacular sunrise views of Mount Everest. You then descend through Pheriche, Tengboche and Namche Bazaar before returning to **Lukla** and flying back to Kathmandu.

**Route at a Glance**

**Kathmandu → Lukla → Phakding → Namche Bazaar → Tengboche → Dingboche → Lobuche → Gorak Shep → Everest Base Camp → Kala Patthar → Pheriche → Namche Bazaar → Lukla → Kathmandu**

The exact route can vary depending on weather, flight schedules, trekking conditions and acclimatization needs.

## Everest Base Camp Trek Permits

To trek to Everest Base Camp, you need **two mandatory permits** — the **Sagarmatha National Park Entry Permit** and the **Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality Permit**. Both are checked at multiple points along the trail.

**Permit Costs**

- **Sagarmatha National Park Entry Permit:** NPR 3,000 (approx. USD 22) for foreign nationals
- **Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality Permit:** NPR 2,000–3,000 (approx. USD 15–23) for foreign nationals
- **Total:** Approximately NPR 5,000–6,000 (around USD 37–46) per person

SAARC nationals pay reduced fees. The national park entry is NPR 1,500 and the municipality fee is NPR 1,000–2,000.

**Where to Get Permits**

- **Sagarmatha National Park Permit:** Available at the Nepal Tourism Board office in Kathmandu or at the Monjo checkpoint on the trail
- **Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality Permit:** Only available at the Lukla checkpoint on arrival or at Monjo. **Not available in Kathmandu**

**No TIMS Card Required**

The TIMS card is **no longer required** for the Everest Base Camp route. The Khumbu region replaced it with its own local permit system in 2018. If a company is still charging you separately for a TIMS card on the EBC route, ask them why.

**Important Notes**

- Carry your **original passport** — photocopies are not accepted at checkpoints
- The national park permit is valid for one entry into the park
- The municipality permit is valid for up to **4 weeks** in the Khumbu region
- Fees are non-refundable and non-transferable once issued.

## Everest Base Camp Trek Elevation

The [**Everest Base Camp Trek**](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/everest-base-camp-trek/) **elevation** is one of the most important things to understand before starting your journey. The trek begins at the lower elevation of Lukla and gradually climbs above 5,000 metres, making proper acclimatization essential. As you move higher through the Khumbu region, the air becomes thinner and the temperature drops, so walking slowly and allowing your body to adjust is important. At **WaveUp Himalayas**, we include appropriate acclimatization days in our trekking itineraries to help trekkers adapt safely to the increasing altitude.

![](/images/blog/ebc_elevation_chart.png)

**Lukla – 2,860 m**

The trek usually begins at **Lukla**, located at approximately 2,860 metres above sea level. From Lukla, the trail gradually climbs through the Khumbu Valley.

**Namche Bazaar – 3,440 m**

**Namche Bazaar** is one of the most important stops for acclimatization. At around 3,440 metres, many trekkers spend an additional night here before continuing toward higher elevations.

**Tengboche – Around 3,860 m**

The trail continues to **Tengboche**, famous for its Buddhist monastery and spectacular views of the surrounding Himalayan peaks.

**Dingboche – Around 4,410 m**

At **Dingboche**, the elevation increases significantly. Most itineraries include an acclimatization day here before continuing toward Lobuche.

**Lobuche – Around 4,940 m**

**Lobuche** is close to 5,000 metres above sea level. At this elevation, the air is noticeably thinner, making a slow walking pace particularly important.

**Gorak Shep – Around 5,164 m**

**Gorak Shep** is one of the highest overnight settlements on the classic Everest Base Camp route. From here, trekkers continue toward Everest Base Camp.

**Everest Base Camp – Around 5,364 m**

The final destination, **Everest Base Camp**, is approximately 5,364 metres above sea level. Reaching this altitude is a major achievement and one of the highlights of the trek.

**Kala Patthar – Around 5,545 m**

**Kala Patthar** is even higher than Everest Base Camp, reaching approximately 5,545 metres. It is famous for its panoramic views of Mount Everest and the surrounding Himalayan peaks.

The rapid increase in elevation is one of the main challenges of the **Everest Base Camp Trek**. At higher altitudes, there is less available oxygen, and your body needs time to adapt.

## Best Time for Everest Base Camp Trek

The **best time for the** [**E**](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/everest-base-camp-trek/)[**verest Base Camp Trek**](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/everest-base-camp-trek/) is generally during **spring (March to May)** and **autumn (September to November)**. These seasons offer more stable weather, clearer mountain views, and better trekking conditions in the Everest region. Autumn, especially **October and November**, is known for clear skies and excellent views of Mount Everest and the surrounding Himalayas. Spring brings warmer days, blooming rhododendrons, and beautiful scenery along the trail.

**Autumn: September to November**

Autumn is one of the most popular times for the **Everest Base Camp Trek**. The weather is generally stable after the monsoon, and the skies are often clear. **October and November** are particularly suitable for trekkers who want excellent Himalayan views and comfortable trekking conditions.

**Spring: March to May**

Spring is another excellent season for the **Everest Base Camp Trek**. The weather becomes warmer, the days are longer, and rhododendron flowers add colour to the lower parts of the trail. March and April are especially popular for trekking in the Everest region.

**Winter: December to February**

Winter brings very cold temperatures and snowfall at higher elevations. However, the skies can still be clear, and the trails are quieter. Winter trekking is possible for experienced trekkers who are properly prepared for cold conditions.

**Monsoon: June to August**

The monsoon season brings heavy rainfall, clouds, and less predictable mountain views. Trails can become wet and slippery, and travel to the Everest region can be affected by weather conditions. For this reason, monsoon is generally not the preferred season for the classic Everest Base Camp Trek.

For the best combination of weather, mountain views, and trekking conditions, we recommend considering autumn or spring. At WaveUp Himalayas, we help you choose the right trekking period based on your schedule, experience, and preferences so you can enjoy your Everest Base Camp adventure with better preparation.

## Everest Base Camp Trek Cost

The[ **Everest Base Camp Trek**](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/everest-base-camp-trek/) **cost** depends on several factors — Lukla flights, permits, guide, porter, teahouse accommodation, meals, insurance, gear, tips, and personal expenses.

Here's a general overview for a 12–14 day trek from Lukla:

![](/images/blog/ebc_trek_cost_table_fixed.png)

Most trekkers budget **$1,800–$2,500** for a comfortable, well-organized EBC trek with a licensed guide and porter.

**What's Included in the Cost**

- **Permits:** \~$45–50 (Sagarmatha National Park + Khumbu Pasang Lhamu)
- **Lukla flights:** $360–$440 round trip
- **Guide:** $25–35/day (mandatory)
- **Porter:** $20–30/day (optional but recommended)
- **Teahouse accommodation:** $5–20 per night
- **Food:** $25–45 per day
- **Travel insurance:** $100–$250 (must cover helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 m)
- **Tips:** $200–$350
- **Extras:** $100–$150 (hot showers, wifi, charging)

**How to Save Money**

- Book with a local Nepali operator (30–50% cheaper than international companies)
- Trek in shoulder season (early March or late November)
- Rent gear in Kathmandu instead of buying
- Join a group trek to split guide and porter costs
- Carry your own daypack to skip the porter
- Use water purification instead of bottled water

**Where NOT to Cut Corners**

- Travel insurance with helicopter evacuation
- Proper boots and layering system
- Acclimatization days
- Licensed guide (mandatory — and could save your life)

When comparing an **Everest Base Camp Trek package**, don't look only at the price. Check what's actually included — permits, flights, teahouses, meals, guide, porter, and insurance.

## Everest Base Camp Trek Difficulty

The [**Everest Base Camp Trek**](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/everest-base-camp-trek/) **difficulty** is considered **challenging but not technical**. No climbing skills, ropes, or mountaineering experience are required. The real challenges are altitude, long walking days, and consecutive days on the trail. Most trekkers rate the EBC trek around **7 out of 10** in difficulty.

![](/images/blog/Copy-of-20220408_080238-scaled.jpg.webp)

**Key Difficulty Factors**

- **Altitude is the main challenge.** At Everest Base Camp (5,364 m), oxygen availability is only about **50% of sea level**.
- **Daily walking:** You will walk roughly **5–7 hours per day**.
- **Total distance:** Approximately **130 km (80 miles) round trip**.
- **Highest point:** **Kala Patthar at 5,545 m**.
- **Trek duration:** Usually **11–14 days of hiking** with only **2–3 acclimatization days**.
- **Fatigue builds up.** You are walking day after day without full recovery.
- **Weather can change quickly.** Cold, wind, snow, and rain are all possible.

**Hardest Sections of the Trek**

- **Climb to Namche Bazaar** — a steep, relentless series of switchbacks.
- **Phunki Tenga to Tengboche** — a sharp drop to the river, then a steep climb back up.
- **Thukla Pass** — short but brutal at high altitude.
- **Lobuche to Gorak Shep** — rocky, steep, and at extreme altitude.
- **Sunrise climb to Kala Patthar** — a 2 a.m. start in freezing darkness.

## Accommodation and Food

Accommodation along the Everest Base Camp route is almost entirely **teahouse-based** — family-run mountain lodges that serve as guesthouse, restaurant and communal living room all in one. There is no camping on the standard route.

![](/images/blog/image_processing20181016-4-n11vub.jpg "Everest Base Camp Tea House")

**Teahouse Accommodation**

- **Room type:** Twin-sharing rooms with a bed, pillow, sheet and blanket
- **Bathrooms:** Often shared; facilities become simpler as you climb higher
- **Lower villages** (Lukla, Phakding, Namche Bazaar): Most comfortable lodges, some with private bathrooms and hot showers
- **Higher villages** (above Tengboche): More basic rooms, thinner walls, foam mattresses
- **Above Lobuche:** Genuinely simple teahouses with shared outdoor facilities
- **Room prices:** NPR 300–1,000 (USD 3–8) per night
- **The teahouse rule:** You are expected to eat where you sleep — this keeps room rates low
- **Extra costs:** Hot showers (USD 2–7), wifi cards (USD 3–15), device charging (USD 1–5)

At **WaveUp Himalayas**, we book teahouses in advance during peak season and know which lodges offer the best food, warmest dining rooms and most reliable facilities at each stop.

**Food on the Trail**

- **Daily food budget:** USD 25–45 per person for breakfast, lunch and dinner
- **Prices rise with altitude** because ingredients must be carried up by porter or yak
- **Common meals:** Dal bhat, noodles, fried rice, pasta, momos, soups, eggs, Tibetan bread, pancakes
- **Best value meal:** Dal bhat — high in calories, nutritious, often with unlimited refills
- **Dal bhat cost:** NPR 700–1,400 (USD 5–10) at mid-altitude; up to NPR 1,600 (USD 12) at Gorak Shep
- **Menu shrinks** as you climb higher; prices increase
- **Drink 3–4 litres of water daily**
- **Use purification tablets or a filter** instead of bottled water — saves money and reduces plastic waste.

From cozy teahouses to hearty dal bhat, accommodation and food on the Everest Base Camp trek are simple but memorable — and an authentic part of the Himalayan experience.

## Everest Base Camp Trek Packing List

Packing is one of the most important parts of preparing for Everest Base Camp — and one of the easiest things to get wrong. Bring too much and you'll struggle under the weight at altitude. Bring too little and you'll be cold, wet and uncomfortable. The key is to pack light, layer smart, and trust that teahouses along the route provide the basics. Aim for a total weight of **8–10 kg** — you'll thank yourself at 5,000 metres.

**Clothing**

- Thermal base layers (2–3 sets)
- Trekking trousers (1–2 pairs)
- Fleece or mid-layer jacket
- Down jacket (rated to -15°C or lower)
- Waterproof/windproof jacket and trousers
- Warm hat, sun hat, gloves, buff
- Trekking socks (4–5 pairs)
- Warm sleeping clothes

**Footwear**

- Waterproof, ankle-supporting trekking boots — broken in before you arrive
- Camp shoes or sandals

**Trekking Gear**

- Daypack (30–40 L) and duffel bag
- Sleeping bag (rated to -10°C or lower)
- Trekking poles, headlamp, sunglasses
- Water bottles and purification tablets or filter
- Backpack rain cover

**Personal Items**

- Sunscreen (SPF 50+) and lip balm
- Toiletries and personal medication
- Basic first-aid kit (blister care, painkillers, Diamox)
- Power bank, camera, passport, Nepalese cash
- Snacks (energy bars, nuts, chocolate)

**What NOT to Bring**

- Cotton clothing
- Heavy books
- Too many electronics
- More than one pair of boots

**Buy vs Rent**

- **Buy at home:** Boots, base layers, socks
- **Rent in Kathmandu:** Down jacket, sleeping bag, trekking poles (USD 25–30 for the whole trek)

A good packing list is not about bringing everything — it's about bringing the right things. Focus on layers, reliable boots and a few essentials, and leave the rest behind.

## Everest Base Camp Trek Safety Tips

Safety should always come first on a high-altitude trek like Everest Base Camp. The mountains are beautiful, but they demand respect. With the right preparation, a sensible pace and a licensed guide by your side, the trek is achievable — and enjoyable. Here are the most important safety tips to keep in mind.

**Altitude Safety**

- **Acclimatize properly.** Above 3,000 m, sleeping altitude should increase by no more than 500 m per day.
- **Take rest days.** The acclimatization days at Namche and Dingboche are not optional.
- **Know the symptoms.** Headache, dizziness, nausea, and loss of appetite are early signs of AMS. Confusion, breathlessness at rest, and coughing are medical emergencies — descend immediately.
- **Consider Diamox.** Consult your doctor before the trek.
- **Never ignore symptoms.** Tell your guide immediately if you feel unwell.

**Pace and Hydration**

- **Walk slowly.** "Slowly, slowly" is the trekking mantra. A steady pace beats a fast start every time.
- **Drink 3–4 litres of water daily.** Hydration helps your body adjust to altitude.
- **Eat regular meals.** Even if you don't feel hungry, eat. Your body needs fuel.
- **Avoid alcohol.** It dehydrates you and worsens altitude symptoms.

**Warmth and Weather**

- **Keep warm and dry.** Change out of wet clothes immediately.
- **Layer up.** Weather can change rapidly — sun, wind, snow, and rain in a single day.
- **Protect against the sun.** UV exposure is intense at altitude. Use sunscreen, lip balm, and sunglasses.
- **Carry a headlamp.** You'll need it for early starts and late arrivals.

**Guide and Communication**

- **Listen to your guide.** They know the trail, the weather, and the signs of altitude sickness.
- **Never trek alone.** Stay with your group.
- **Carry a charged phone.** Network coverage is limited but available in some villages.
- **Share your itinerary.** Leave a copy with someone at home.

**Insurance and Emergency**

- **Get proper insurance.** Your policy must cover helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 m.
- **Carry cash.** ATMs are unreliable above Namche.
- **Know the emergency numbers.** Your guide will have them.

**Schedule Buffer**

- **Build in extra days.** Lukla flights are frequently delayed or cancelled due to weather.
- **Don't rush your itinerary.** A rushed trek increases the risk of altitude sickness.

The Everest Base Camp trek is safe when approached with respect, preparation, and the right support. Walk slowly, drink plenty of water, listen to your body, and trust your guide.
