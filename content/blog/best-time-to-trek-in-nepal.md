Nepal doesn't have a single "best" trekking season — it has several good ones, each with its own personality. Understanding how the seasons differ will help you pick dates that suit the trail, the views and the pace you're after.

## Spring (March to May)

Spring is one of the most popular windows for trekking in the Himalayas. As the hills warm up, rhododendron forests burst into bloom along many lower and mid-altitude trails, and daytime temperatures are comfortable for walking. Mornings tend to be clearer, with haze sometimes building up by afternoon.

- Warmer days, cold nights at higher elevations
- Rhododendron and magnolia blooms on forested trails
- Long daylight hours for full trekking days
- Popular routes can feel busier, especially around peak weeks

## Autumn (September to November)

Autumn is generally considered the clearest and most stable season, arriving right after the monsoon has washed the dust out of the sky. Visibility is often excellent, humidity drops, and the weather is typically dry and settled — which is part of why so many trekkers plan their trips for these months.

> Clear skies and stable weather are never guaranteed anywhere in the mountains, but autumn's post-monsoon air is often the closest thing to it.

## Winter (December to February)

Winter brings cold temperatures and snow to high passes, which can close some routes or make them significantly more demanding. Lower-altitude treks, however, remain very doable and reward you with quiet trails and crisp, clear mountain views, since fewer trekkers are on the trail.

## Monsoon / Summer (June to August)

The monsoon brings regular rainfall to most of the country, along with leeches on lower trails and clouds that can obscure mountain views. That said, trans-Himalayan regions in the rain shadow — such as Upper Mustang and parts of the Manaslu and Dolpo areas — stay comparatively dry and are a good option if you want to trek during these months.

## A few general planning tips

- Build a couple of buffer days into any itinerary for weather delays, especially on flights to mountain airstrips.
- Higher passes and viewpoints are colder and windier than valley floors — pack layers accordingly.
- Trail conditions can vary year to year, so always check current conditions with your guide before departure.
- Shoulder-season weeks (just before or after the main season) can offer a good balance of decent weather and fewer crowds.
