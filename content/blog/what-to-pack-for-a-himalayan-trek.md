Packing for a Himalayan trek is less about bringing everything you own and more about bringing the right layers. Trails typically move you through several climate zones in a single day, so a flexible system beats a single heavy jacket.

## Layering, not bulk

The most reliable approach is three simple layers that you can add or remove as the trail climbs and descends:

- A moisture-wicking base layer to manage sweat on the climbs
- A warm mid layer (fleece or light insulated jacket) for breaks and colder stretches
- A windproof, waterproof outer shell for wind, rain or snow

Add a warmer insulated jacket for evenings at higher elevations, where temperatures drop quickly once the sun goes down.

## Footwear and legwear

Comfortable, broken-in trekking boots matter more than almost anything else on this list — this is not the trip to try out brand-new boots. Pair them with a few pairs of good trekking socks, and consider gaiters if you expect snow or muddy trails.

## General kit checklist

- Sturdy, worn-in trekking boots + camp shoes or sandals for evenings
- Sleeping bag rated for the season and altitude of your route
- Trekking poles for knee support on long descents
- A headlamp with spare batteries
- Reusable water bottle and a means of water purification
- Sun hat, sunglasses with good UV protection, and a warm hat/beanie
- Sunscreen and lip balm — UV exposure is stronger at altitude
- A basic first-aid kit and any personal medication
- A dry bag or liner to keep gear dry inside your pack

> Altitude sun is deceptively strong even on cool, cloudy days — sunscreen and good sunglasses are not optional extras.

## Documents and money

Carry printed and digital copies of your permits, insurance details and identification, plus some local cash in smaller denominations — cards and ATMs are unreliable once you're away from the larger towns.

## Pack light, pack smart

Most teahouse treks limit porter loads, so it pays to lay everything out before you leave, then remove anything you can genuinely live without. A lighter pack means a more enjoyable trail every single day of the trip.
