## Do You Need Permits for the Everest Base Camp Trek?

Yes. **You need exactly two permits** for the standard Lukla route, and no more. The two mandatory documents are:

1. **Sagarmatha National Park Entry Permit**
2. **Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality Permit** (also called the Khumbu Trek Card)

Together, these **Everest Base Camp permit fees 2026** total **NPR 6,000 (approximately USD 39)** for most foreign nationals. For SAARC nationals, the total is **NPR 3,500 (approximately USD 23)**.

### What to Carry When Buying Permits

At both the Kathmandu NTB office and the trail counters, you will need:

- **Your original passport** — photocopies are not accepted at checkpoints.
- **Passport-sized photos** — a few spare copies are always useful.
- **Cash in Nepalese Rupees** — both permits are paid in NPR, and trail ATMs are unreliable.

Your permits will be **stamped at Monjo** on your way up, and the stamp is **checked again on your descent**. Carry the originals with you at all times—not in your checked duffel or with a porter.

### The TIMS Card Myth—Debunked

If you've been searching for **"TIMS card Everest Base Camp,"** you're not alone. Many older guides still list it. Here's the truth: **the TIMS card is NOT required for the Everest Base Camp trek**. The Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality withdrew from the national TIMS system in 2018 and introduced its own local permit instead. If a trekking agency tries to charge you for a TIMS card on this route, ask questions—you likely don't need it.

## The Two Mandatory Permits: A Complete Breakdown

### Permit 1: Sagarmatha National Park Entry Permit (SNP)

This is your ticket into the UNESCO World Heritage-listed national park that surrounds Everest. The **Sagarmatha National Park entry permit** is required for every trekker entering the protected area, which covers roughly 1,148 square kilometers of Himalayan terrain—including Namche Bazaar, Tengboche, Dingboche, Lobuche, and Everest Base Camp itself.

**Cost (2026)**

The **Sagarmatha National Park entry fee** is **NPR 3,000 for foreign nationals** and **NPR 1,500 for SAARC nationals** (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, and Afghanistan). Nepali citizens pay a nominal NPR 100, and children under 10 enter free.

**A note on VAT:** Some sources indicate a 13% VAT may be added on top of the base fee at certain counters, bringing the effective cost closer to **NPR 3,390 (approximately USD 25–28)** for foreign nationals. At WaveUp Himalayas, we always budget for the upper end so there are no surprises at the counter.

**Where to Buy the Sagarmatha National Park Permit**

You have two convenient options:

1. **In Kathmandu (recommended):** Visit the **Nepal Tourism Board (NTB) office** at Bhrikutimandap before you fly to Lukla. This is the smoothest option and avoids any last-minute scrambling on the trail.
2. **On the trail:** Purchase it at the **Monjo checkpoint**, which you’ll pass through roughly two days after starting from Lukla.

**Validity**

The SNP permit is valid for **one trek entry** into the national park. It covers your entire journey from Monjo through to Gorakshep and Kala Patthar—there are no daily limits or additional charges along the route.

### Permit 2: Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality Permit (Khumbu Trek Card)

This is the local permit that **replaced the TIMS card** in 2018. The Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality withdrew from the national TIMS system and introduced its own fee, arguing that TIMS money raised in the district was not coming back to the local community. Today, this **Khumbu Trek Card** is mandatory for all trekkers entering the Khumbu region.

**Cost (2026)** 

The **Khumbu Pasang Lhamu permit price** is the murkiest part of the entire system. Here is what the named 2026 sources actually print:

- **NPR 3,000 (approximately USD 20–23)** — cited by Nepal Hiking Team, Ace the Himalaya, Glorious Himalaya, and Mountain Hike Nepal.
- **NPR 2,000 (approximately USD 15)** — cited by Prime Himalayas and Mount Elegance.

For **SAARC nationals**, the fee is generally **NPR 2,000 (approximately USD 13)**.

**Our advice at WaveUp Himalayas:** Budget for **NPR 3,000** as the safer figure. The fee has trended upward, and multiple recent guides confirm the higher amount. Carry a little extra cash and verify the current number at the counter—the discrepancy is real, and it’s better to be over-prepared than caught short.

**Where to Get the Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Permit**

This is the critical difference from the SNP permit: **the Khumbu permit is NOT available in Kathmandu.** It must be purchased **on the trail only**:

- At the **municipality counter in Lukla** upon arrival, or
- At the **Monjo checkpoint**, alongside the national park permit.

If you are trekking independently, plan to buy this permit in Lukla before you start walking. If you are trekking with **WaveUp Himalayas**, we arrange everything before you fly, and your guide carries the paperwork—so you never have to queue at a counter.

**Validity**

The Khumbu permit is valid for **up to 4 weeks** in the Khumbu region. For the standard 12–14 day EBC trek, this is more than sufficient.

## Where Are Permits Checked on the Trail?

Your permits are sorted—now, where exactly will you need to show them? The good news is that the checking process on the Everest Base Camp trail is straightforward and predictable. There are two primary checkpoints you will encounter on the way up, with a verification on the way down. Here is the complete picture.

### Checkpoint 1: Lukla (Day 1) — The Khumbu Permit

![](/images/blog/LUAD4648.jpg "Lukla Airport")

Your first permit encounter happens almost immediately. On **Day 1**, upon arrival at **Lukla Airport** (altitude 2,860m), you will pass through the **Lukla arrival checkpoint**. This is where the **Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality Permit** is checked and registered. If you are trekking independently and have not yet purchased this permit, this is also where you can buy it at the municipality counter.

The process is quick—typically **2 to 5 minutes per trekker**. The officer verifies your identity against your **original passport** and records your permit number. Beyond showing your passport when asked, no action is required from you.

### Checkpoint 2: Monjo / Jorsale (Day 2) — Both Permits Stamped

![](/images/blog/e41a544cb956226279bd4f3ebe9ad13a-large.webp "Monjo CheckPoint")

The main event happens on **Day 2**, roughly two days' walk from Lukla. You will pass through the **Monjo checkpoint** (also referred to as the Jorsale checkpoint), which is the official **entry gate to Sagarmatha National Park**, situated between Phakding and Namche Bazaar.

Here, **both of your permits are stamped** on the way up—the **Sagarmatha National Park Entry Permit** and the **Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality Permit**. This is the critical checkpoint where your paperwork is officially validated for the upper Khumbu valley.

**A note on the Monjo queue:** During peak trekking season, the queue at Monjo can be significant. If you are trekking with **WaveUp Himalayas**, your guide handles the presentation and you walk through without delay. Independent trekkers should budget extra time here.

### Additional Internal Checkpoints

Beyond Lukla and Monjo, permits are **occasionally checked at internal checkpoints** further up the valley. You may encounter verification at places like **Dingboche, Lobuche, or Gorak Shep**, though these are generally less formal than the main Monjo gate. The key is to always carry your originals—not in your duffel or with a porter.

### The Descent: Stamp Verification

Your permit is not a one-time ticket. The **stamp you receive at Monjo on the way up is checked again on your way down**. This confirms you exited the park legally and helps authorities track trekkers on the route.

## The Jiri Approach: An Additional Permit

If you are flying into Lukla, the standard two-permit system is all you need. But if you are taking the **classic Jiri approach**—the historic overland route that Hillary and Tenzing’s era trekkers walked—there is one more document to add to your folder: the **Gaurishankar Conservation Area Permit (GCAP)**. This is the additional permit that catches out trekkers who assume the Jiri route uses the same paperwork as the Lukla flight.

![](/images/blog/images%20%2810%29.jpg "Jiri")

**Why it is required:** The Jiri trail begins outside the Khumbu region and passes through the **Gaurishankar Conservation Area** before eventually joining the Sagarmatha National Park at Namche Bazaar. The GCAP covers your entry into that protected zone for the **Jiri-to-Namche section** of the walk. It is not needed if you fly to Lukla or start by road from Salleri, Phaplu, or Surke—those approaches follow the standard two-permit rules.

**The cost:** The GCAP fee is **NPR 3,000 for other foreign nationals**, **NPR 3,000 for Chinese nationals**, and **NPR 1,000 for SAARC nationals**. This is **on top of** the standard Sagarmatha National Park permit (NPR 3,000) and the Khumbu Pasang Lhamu permit (NPR 3,000). That brings the **total permit cost for the traditional Jiri route to NPR 9,000 (approximately USD 60–65) for most foreign nationals**—compared to NPR 6,000 on the Lukla route.

**Where to get it:** Unlike the Khumbu permit, the GCAP **can be purchased in Kathmandu**. Visit the **Nepal Tourism Board office at Bhrikutimandap** before you head to Jiri. Bring your original passport, a passport-sized photo, and cash in Nepalese Rupees. The permit is valid for the Jiri-to-Namche section and is checked at the conservation area entry points before you reach the Khumbu.

**The bottom line:** The Jiri approach is a longer, quieter, and more immersive route to Everest—but it comes with a third permit. If you are walking from Jiri, budget for the **Gaurishankar Conservation Area Permit** alongside the standard two, and secure it in Kathmandu before you leave. **WaveUp Himalayas** arranges all three permits for trekkers on the classic Jiri itinerary, so you can step onto the trail with your paperwork complete.
