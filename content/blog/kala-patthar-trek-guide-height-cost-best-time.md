**Kala Patthar (5,545 m / 18,192 ft) is the best viewpoint for Mount Everest on the Everest Base Camp trek.** This dark rocky ridge rises above Gorak Shep in Nepal's Khumbu region, and the climb takes about 2 to 3 hours. Unlike Everest Base Camp itself, which has no view of the summit, Kala Patthar gives you a clear, close look at Everest, Nuptse, Lhotse and Pumori, especially at sunrise.

In this complete guide, WaveUp Himalayas explains everything you need to plan your Kala Patthar trek: the height, the best month to go, the 13-day itinerary, trek cost from Kathmandu, permits, altitude sickness tips and what to pack. Whether you are a first-time trekker or planning your next Himalayan adventure, you will find clear answers from a licensed, local team based in Kathmandu.

## Where is Kala Patthar located, and why is it called "black rock"?

![Kala Patthar](/images/blog/kalapatthar-mount-everest-sunrise-view-everest-base-camp-trek-001.jpg "Kala Patthar")

Kala Patthar sits in the Khumbu region of Nepal, inside [Sagarmatha National Park](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/sagarmatha-national-park/). It is a small ridge on the southern flank of Pumori, rising above the settlement of Gorak Shep, the last overnight stop on the Everest Base Camp trek.

The name is Nepali and Hindi for "black rock" (you will also see it spelled Kalapatthar, Kala Pathar or Kalapathar). The ridge looks dark because the rock is bare and often free of snow, which makes it stand out against the white giants around it. It is not a summit in its own right, but it is the most famous viewpoint in the Himalayas.

***

## What can you see from Kala Patthar?

This is the reason people climb it. Everest Base Camp itself sits low in a glacial valley and has no view of the summit. From Kala Patthar you get a clear line to Mount Everest and a full circle of other giants:

- Mount Everest, with its dark pyramid summit rising behind the Nuptse-Lhotse wall
- Nuptse and Lhotse, the great ridge that frames Everest
- Pumori, the sharp peak directly above you
- Changtse, the northern neighbour of Everest
- The Khumbu Glacier winding below, with the Everest Base Camp area at its edge

On a clear morning it is the kind of view that makes the long walk feel worth it. Many trekkers tell us that this, not base camp, was the highlight of the trip.

## Kala Patthar height in feet and meters

![ Kala Patthar 5,545 m (18,192 ft)](/images/blog/kala-patthar-banner-001.jpg " Kala Patthar 5,545 m (18,192 ft)")

The commonly cited Kala Patthar altitude is 5,545 m (about 18,192 ft). You may see figures up to roughly 5,644 m in other sources, because heights in the Khumbu vary slightly between surveys and GPS readings. We use 5,545 m on our itineraries.

That makes Kala Patthar higher than Everest Base Camp (5,364 m), and it is the highest point most Everest Base Camp trekkers will ever reach. Gorak Shep, where you start the climb, sits at about 5,164 m, so the hike itself gains roughly 380 m.

***

## Kala Patthar vs Everest Base Camp: which is better?

They are not rivals, they are two halves of the same experience. Base camp gives you the sense of arrival, the glacier, the tents and the Khumbu Icefall. Kala Patthar gives you the view of Everest. Almost every Everest Base Camp trek with Kala Patthar includes both.

|  | Everest Base Camp | Kala Patthar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Altitude | 5,364 m | 5,545 m |
| View of Everest summit | No | Yes |
| Main appeal | Reaching the base camp itself | Panoramic view of Everest, Nuptse and Lhotse |
| Starting point | Gorak Shep | Gorak Shep |
| Best time of day | Late morning | Sunrise or sunset |

If you only have energy for one, choose Kala Patthar for the view. If you want the full story, do both.

## How long does it take to hike Kala Patthar from Gorak Shep?

![Gorak Shep Village](/images/blog/Gorakshep-Village-001.jpg "Gorak Shep Village")

Plan for roughly 2 to 3 hours to climb from Gorak Shep to the top, and about 1 to 1.5 hours to come back down. The path is clear but steep, with loose rock near the upper section, and every step at this altitude feels slower than it looks.

For a sunrise hike, most groups leave Gorak Shep in the dark, around 4:00 to 5:00 AM depending on the season, with headlamps. Walk slowly, keep a steady rhythm and take short breaks. Your guide will set the pace so you reach the top before the first light hits Everest.

***

## Kala Patthar sunrise vs sunset: which is better?

![Kala Patthar sunrise view](/images/blog/Sunrise-view-from-Kala-Patthar-001.webp "Kala Patthar sunrise view")

Both are spectacular, and the choice comes down to cold, crowds and how you feel.

- **Sunrise:** Golden light on Everest and Nuptse, usually the clearest skies, but you start in the dark and stand in the coldest hour of the day. It is also the busiest.
- **Sunset:** Quieter, with a softer pink glow and a warmer climb. The downside is that you come down in fading light, and afternoon cloud can roll in.

Our advice: go for sunrise if you slept well and feel strong. If you are tired or the weather looks uncertain, sunset is a good plan B. We often build a spare day into the schedule so you have a second chance.

***

## Best month to trek to Kala Patthar: is November a good time?

The two best windows are autumn (late September to November) and spring (March to May). Skies are usually clearest in October and November, which is why this is peak season for views.

November in particular offers crisp, stable weather and excellent visibility, but it is cold, especially at Kala Patthar at dawn. By late November the trails get quieter and nights drop sharply. Spring 2027 is a good alternative: warmer, with rhododendrons in bloom lower down.

December to February is possible for well-prepared trekkers, but expect extreme cold and some closed lodges. June to August is monsoon, with heavy cloud that often hides the mountains.

## Everest Base Camp and Kala Patthar trek: 13-day itinerary

![Everest Base Camp](/images/blog/1653287230_ebc-c5hp0XiZDKvr6pLMNSUCPm0ibYD9kQnZDoIR3DU8-002.jpg "Everest Base Camp")

Our [13-day Everest Base Camp Trek](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/everest-base-camp-trek/) runs from Kathmandu to base camp at 5,364 m and Kala Patthar at 5,545 m. Here is the typical flow of days. We tailor dates, rest days and pace to your group.

1. Arrive in Kathmandu, transfer to your hotel and meet your guide.
2. Fly to Lukla and trek to Phakding.
3. Trek to Namche Bazaar, the Sherpa capital of the Khumbu.
4. Acclimatization day in Namche, with a short hike higher and back.
5. Trek to Tengboche and its famous monastery.
6. Trek to Dingboche.
7. Acclimatization day in Dingboche.
8. Trek to Lobuche.
9. Trek to Gorak Shep, then on to Everest Base Camp and back.
10. **Kala Patthar at sunrise, then descend to a lower village.**
11. Trek back toward Namche Bazaar.
12. Trek to Lukla.
13. Fly to Kathmandu.

The two acclimatization days are not optional extras. They are what make the summit-day view at Kala Patthar possible for most people. If you want more time or a longer route, ask us about the Everest Base Camp trek with Gokyo Lakes or the Three Passes.

There is no separate permit for Kala Patthar. It is covered by the permits for the Everest Base Camp route:

## How to avoid altitude sickness at Kala Patthar

Altitude is the real challenge here, not the distance. At 5,545 m there is roughly half the oxygen available at sea level, and anyone can be affected regardless of fitness. These habits matter most:

- **Go slowly.** The guide's favourite words on this trail are "bistarai, bistarai" (slowly, slowly).
- **Respect the rest days.** Do not skip the acclimatization days in Namche and Dingboche.
- **Hydrate.** Drink plenty of fluids through the day and avoid alcohol.
- **Climb high, sleep low.** Short hikes above your sleeping altitude help your body adapt.
- **Speak up early.** Headache, nausea, dizziness or unusual tiredness are warning signs. Tell your guide at once.
- **Be ready to descend.** If symptoms get worse, going down is the cure. No view is worth a risk.

If you are considering medication such as acetazolamide, speak to your doctor before you travel. We cannot give dosage advice, and it is a medical decision. Good travel insurance with high-altitude cover is essential.

## Kala Patthar trek packing list for November (and how cold it gets)

Expect temperatures well below freezing at Kala Patthar at sunrise, often around -15°C or colder once wind chill is added. Layers are everything. A practical list:

- Warm down jacket rated for extreme cold
- Base layers, fleece mid-layer and a windproof, waterproof shell
- Insulated gloves with thin liners, warm hat, buff and sunglasses with strong UV protection
- Broken-in trekking boots and warm socks
- Headlamp with spare batteries (essential for a sunrise start)
- Sleeping bag suitable for cold teahouse nights
- Trekking poles, water bottles and sun cream
- Basic first-aid kit, your own medication and personal documents

Keep camera batteries inside your jacket, since the cold drains them fast. We share a full gear checklist when you book.

## Is the Kala Patthar trek hard for first-time trekkers?

It is demanding but achievable for a reasonably fit beginner with enough time. You do not need climbing skills, ropes or crampons. What you need is stamina for long walking days, patience with the altitude and a good schedule with rest days.

Most of our trekkers are first-timers in Nepal. Start training a few weeks ahead with long walks, stairs and cardio, ideally with a loaded daypack.

**Can you trek without a guide or go solo?** Independent trekking is still possible, but a guide makes a real difference on this route: weather decisions, altitude monitoring, accommodation in busy season and safety if something goes wrong. Solo travellers often join us for exactly this reason. We also offer a dedicated female-guided option for women travelling alone.

## Kala Patthar vs Gokyo Ri, Poon Hill and Tiger Hill

Travellers often ask how Kala Patthar compares with other famous viewpoints.

| Viewpoint | Altitude | Region | What you see | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kala Patthar | 5,545 m | Everest | Everest summit, Nuptse, Lhotse, Pumori | Hard (altitude) |
| Gokyo Ri | about 5,357 m | Everest | Everest, Cho Oyu, Gokyo Lakes | Hard (altitude) |
| Poon Hill | 3,210 m | Annapurna | Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges | Easy to moderate |
| Tiger Hill | about 2,590 m | Darjeeling, India | Kanchenjunga at sunrise | Easy (drive-up) |

Kala Patthar gives the closest and most dramatic view of Everest. Gokyo Ri is a stunning alternative with turquoise lakes, and it can be combined with base camp. Poon Hill is a gentler first Himalayan sunrise, and our [Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/ghorepani-poon-hill-trek/) is ideal if you have fewer days.

## Kala Patthar photography tips

- Arrive early and claim a spot on the upper rocks before the light turns golden.
- Use a tripod or a steady rock, and shoot in low light with a slow shutter rather than raising ISO too high.
- Take wide shots for the Nuptse-Lhotse wall and tighter frames for Everest, whose summit looks smaller than people expect.
- Include prayer flags and fellow trekkers for scale.
- Keep spare batteries warm in an inside pocket, and bring a lens cloth for frost.
- Put the camera down for a few minutes. The moment is worth seeing without a screen.
