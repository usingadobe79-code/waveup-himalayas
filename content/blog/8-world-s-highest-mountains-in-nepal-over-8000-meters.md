Nepal is home to eight of the world's fourteen **eight-thousanders**, a remarkable concentration of **Nepal 8000m peaks** that includes Everest, Kanchenjunga, Lhotse, Makalu, Cho Oyu, Dhaulagiri, Manaslu, and Annapurna I—collectively the **highest mountains in Nepal**. These **8000m mountains in Nepal** span six major ranges, and while many travelers ask **how many 8000m peaks in Nepal** exist and which ones are **fully in Nepal** (Dhaulagiri, Manaslu, and Annapurna I lie entirely within its borders), the full **list of 8000m peaks in Nepal** reveals everything from the accessible slopes of Cho Oyu to the notoriously dangerous faces of Annapurna I. Whether you are researching a **Nepal 8000m peaks map** for a future **Nepal 8000m peak expedition**, planning a base camp trek beneath these giants, or simply dreaming of standing on the roof of the world, this guide from **Wave Up Himalayas** will help you understand the scale, location, and character of Nepal's **8 eight-thousanders**.

## How Many 8000m Peaks Are in Nepal?

The answer depends on who you ask. The globally recognized count is **eight** — these are the peaks that appear on every standard list of eight-thousanders and are universally accepted as independent mountains. However, in early 2025, Nepal's Department of Tourism updated its official peak profile to recognize **fourteen** mountains above 8,000 meters, adding six subsidiary summits such as Yalung Kang and Lhotse Middle to the national tally. For climbers and trekkers planning expeditions, the classic eight remain the primary reference points. When you search for a **list of 8000m peaks in Nepal**, these are the names you will encounter.

### The List of 8000m Peaks in Nepal

**Mount Everest (8,848.86 m)**

![](/images/blog/1596997438-001.jpg "Mount Everest (8,848.86 m)")

The highest mountain in Nepal and the world, Everest (Sagarmatha in Nepali) needs little introduction. Located in the Mahalangur Himal on the Nepal–China border, it is the ultimate objective for high-altitude mountaineers. The standard South Col route from Nepal is the most popular, though it demands serious preparation and a lengthy acclimatization schedule. For trekkers, **[Everest Base Camp](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/everest-base-camp-trek/)** remains one of Nepal's most iconic journeys.

**Kanchenjunga (8,586 m)**

![](/images/blog/Kangchenjunga_PangPema.jpg "Kanchenjunga (8,586 m)")

The third-highest mountain in the world sits on the Nepal–India border in the far eastern Himalaya. Kanchenjunga sees far fewer expeditions than Everest, making it an attractive option for climbers seeking solitude and a more self-sufficient experience. The surrounding Kanchenjunga Conservation Area also offers superb trekking routes for those who prefer to admire the peak from below.

**Lhotse (8,516 m)**

![](/images/blog/image-2-e1748338483564-1024x727.jpg "Lhotse (8,516 m)")

Connected to Everest via the South Col, Lhotse is the fourth-highest mountain in the world. Many Everest expeditions share their approach and base camp logistics with Lhotse, though the climbing itself is more technical. It is often considered by experienced mountaineers looking to add a second eight-thousander to their résumé.

**Makalu (8,463 m)**

![](/images/blog/MakaluExpedition.jpg "Makalu (8,463 m)")

The fifth-highest peak in the world, Makalu is renowned for its striking pyramidal shape and technical difficulty. Located on the Nepal–China border, it receives fewer climbers than Everest or Cho Oyu, making it a serious undertaking for well-prepared expeditions.

**Cho Oyu (8,201 m)**

![](/images/blog/images%20%2814%29.jpg "Cho Oyu (8,201 m)")

Cho Oyu is widely regarded as one of the more accessible eight-thousanders in Nepal. Its location on the Nepal–China border and relatively straightforward northwest ridge make it a popular choice for climbers attempting their first 8,000-meter peak. That said, "accessible" is a relative term — Cho Oyu still demands excellent fitness, acclimatization, and mountaineering skills.

**Dhaulagiri I (8,167 m)**

![](/images/blog/Dhaulagiri-Expedition-6.jpg "Dhaulagiri I (8,167 m)")

Dhaulagiri I is the seventh-highest mountain in the world and lies **entirely within Nepal**. Its name means "White Mountain" in Sanskrit, and its steep, dramatic faces are visible from the popular Annapurna region. Climbing Dhaulagiri is a significant step up in commitment and technical difficulty compared to Cho Oyu.

**Manaslu (8,163 m)**

![](/images/blog/Manaslu%2BExpedition-001.jpg "Manaslu (8,163 m)")

Manaslu, the "Mountain of Spirit," is the eighth-highest peak in the world and another that lies **fully within Nepal**. It has become a favored choice for climbers preparing for Everest, as it offers a genuine eight-thousander experience with somewhat less traffic than the Khumbu giants. The **[Manaslu Circuit trek](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/manaslu-circuit-trek/)** is also one of Nepal's finest teahouse treks.

**Annapurna I (8,091 m)**

![](/images/blog/20121217194414-184bcb82-me-001.jpg "Annapurna I (8,091 m)")

Annapurna I holds a fearsome reputation. It was the first eight-thousander to be climbed, in 1950, but it also has one of the highest fatality rates among all 14 peaks. The standard north face route is safer than the notorious south face, but Annapurna remains a serious objective for highly experienced mountaineers only.

## Which 8000m Peaks Are Fully in Nepal?

One of the most common questions from travelers researching **which 8000m peaks are fully in Nepal** is whether they can see or approach these mountains without crossing into Tibet or India. Of Nepal's eight eight-thousanders, only **three** lie entirely within Nepalese territory:

- **Dhaulagiri I** (8,167 m) — the seventh-highest mountain in the world, located in the Dhaulagiri Himal of central Nepal
- **Manaslu** (8,163 m) — the eighth-highest mountain in the world, in the Mansiri Himal of Gorkha district
- **Annapurna I** (8,091 m) — the tenth-highest mountain in the world, in the Annapurna Himal of central Nepal

The remaining five are shared:

- **Everest, Lhotse, Makalu, and Cho Oyu** sit on the **Nepal–China (Tibet) border**
- **Kanchenjunga** sits on the **Nepal–India (Sikkim) border**

For trekkers, this distinction matters less than you might think. All eight peaks are accessible from the Nepalese side for base camp treks and expeditions, and the border peaks can be climbed via Nepalese routes with the appropriate permits. The three "fully in Nepal" peaks, however, offer a particular advantage: their approaches and base camps are entirely within Nepal, and treks like the Annapurna Base Camp and Manaslu Circuit bring you deep into their home ranges without any border-crossing logistics.

If you want to experience an 8000m peak entirely on Nepalese soil—from trailhead to base camp—Dhaulagiri, Manaslu, and Annapurna I are your mountains.

## Easiest and Hardest 8000m Peaks in Nepal

For climbers eyeing their first or final eight-thousander, the question of difficulty is inevitable. Among Nepal's eight peaks, the range is extreme—from relatively accessible objectives to mountains with fatality rates that demand serious reflection.

### Cho Oyu and Manaslu as First 8000ers

For those asking about the **easiest 8000m peak in Nepal**, **Cho Oyu** is the traditional answer. Its standard northwest ridge is widely regarded as the least technically demanding route on any Nepalese eight-thousander, with lower-angle slopes and a more straightforward climbing line . However, access has become a practical concern: Cho Oyu's standard route approaches from Tibet, and foreign climbing access there has been restricted in recent seasons, meaning permits and logistics must be confirmed before planning .

**Manaslu** has emerged as the practical alternative for a first Nepalese eight-thousander. It offers Nepal's mature expedition infrastructure—strong Sherpa support, established logistics, and reliable helicopter rescue capability—while remaining technically more accessible than most of its neighbours . It is often climbed as preparation for Everest the following spring . The caveat is crowding: Manaslu has become increasingly commercial, with summit-push bottlenecks a real concern .

### Annapurna I and Dhaulagiri for Experienced Climbers

At the opposite end, **Annapurna I is statistically the deadliest eight-thousander in the world**, with a death rate of roughly one for every three summiteers . This is not because its standard route is exceptionally technical—it is the objective hazard that kills. Massive seracs loom above almost the entire route up to Camp 3, and there is essentially no safe path through the avalanche exposure . Annapurna should only be attempted by climbers with extensive high-altitude experience and a clear-eyed acceptance of the risk.

**Dhaulagiri I** also sits on the harder end of Nepal's list. Its fatality rate is lower than Annapurna's but still significant, at roughly one death for every six to seven summiteers . The mountain has a history of avalanches on its ridge routes, and the climbing is a substantial step up in commitment and technical difficulty compared to Manaslu or Cho Oyu .

**Kanchenjunga**, while not the deadliest statistically, demands greater self-sufficiency and fewer crowds than the popular peaks—it receives far fewer expeditions, meaning less fixed rope, less traffic, and a more traditional expedition experience.

## Nepal 8000m Peak Expeditions: What to Expect

Planning a **Nepal 8000m peak expedition** is a serious undertaking, and understanding what lies ahead—from permits to logistics to safety protocols—will help you prepare realistically. Here is what climbers can expect when attempting an eight-thousander in Nepal.

**Climbing Seasons**

The primary season for **Nepal 8000m peak expeditions** is spring (late March to May), when weather windows are most stable. A secondary autumn season (September–November) sees fewer expeditions but quieter conditions. Winter and summer climbs are rare and reserved for highly experienced mountaineers.

**Permits and Logistics**

All eight-thousander expeditions require permits from the Department of Tourism. Everest's spring royalty fee rose to **$15,000\*\* in September 2025, while other 8000m peaks cost \*\*$3,000** in spring and $1,500 in autumn. Solo climbing is no longer permitted above 8,000 meters—each pair of climbers must be accompanied by at least one guide, and GPS devices and rescue insurance are mandatory.

**Safety and Sherpa Support**

Above 8,000 meters, oxygen levels are insufficient for sustained human function. Professional Sherpa support, supplemental oxygen, and rigorous acclimatization are non-negotiable. For peaks other than Everest, Nepal requires one Sherpa for every two climbers. Acclimatization rotations—ascending to higher camps and descending multiple times—are standard practice before any summit push.

**What an Expedition Looks Like**

A typical 8000m expedition runs **six to eight weeks**, including approach, acclimatization, and summit attempts. Costs vary widely: a full-service Manaslu expedition ranges from **$10,500 to $20,000**, while Everest averages around **$60,000**.
