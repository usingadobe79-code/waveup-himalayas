  The **Everest Base Camp trek with Gokyo Lakes** is a 17-day guided circuit that joins Nepal's most famous trail to its most beautiful quiet valley. After the scenic [flight to Lukla](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/flight-to-lukla-safety-delays-what-to-expect/), you trek through Sherpa villages and [Sagarmatha National Park](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/sagarmatha-national-park/), climb the Gokyo valley to its turquoise glacial lakes, summit **Gokyo Ri (5,357 m)**, cross the **Cho La Pass (5,420 m)**, and stand at **Everest Base Camp (5,364 m)** and **Kala Patthar (5,545 m)**.

  Also known as the Everest Circuit trek, or the Everest Base Camp, Gokyo and Cho La trek, this route adds four days to our 13-day [Everest Base Camp Trek](https://waveuphimalayas.com/packages/everest-base-camp-trek/). In return you get a sunrise panorama of Everest, Lhotse, Makalu and Cho Oyu from Gokyo Ri, quieter trails in the Gokyo valley, and a loop through two valleys instead of one up-and-back walk. preparation: |-
  The Everest Base Camp trek with Gokyo Lakes is one of the most rewarding routes in the Khumbu, but it asks for respect for the altitude, the cold and the local culture. Here is what to know, prepare for and expect. For costs, permits and packing in one place, read our [Everest Base Camp trek guide](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/everest-base-camp-trek-guide-cost-permits-packing-altitude-more/).

  ### **Altitude, Fitness & Acclimatization**

  - **Maximum Altitude:** 5,545 m at Kala Patthar. Cho La reaches 5,420 m and the highest night is at Gorak Shep (5,164 m), where the air holds roughly half the oxygen of sea level.
  - **Acclimatization is Critical:** Two rest days (Namche and Gokyo) and a steady pace keep the gains manageable. The biggest jump is Day 3 (+830 m), followed straight away by a rest day. Sleeping at Dole on Day 5 keeps Days 5 to 7 to +600, +430 and +320 m, and you spend five nights between 4,000 and 4,800 m before the pass. After Kala Patthar you sleep about 800 m lower. [LINK: altitude sickness on the Everest trek]
  - **Fitness Level:** Good fitness is recommended, as the trek involves 3 to 9 hours of walking per day at high altitude. Start cardio and strength training 8 to 12 weeks before departure, and practice with a loaded daypack (7–10 kg) on hills or long staircases on consecutive days. Previous multi-day trekking experience is strongly advised because of the Cho La day; first-timers can read [how difficult the Everest Base Camp trek is](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/how-difficult-is-everest-base-camp-trek/) and the [mistakes to avoid as a first-time trekker](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/mistakes-to-avoid-as-first-time-trekker/).
  - **Altitude Sickness (AMS):** Know the symptoms (headache, nausea, dizziness) and tell your guide immediately. We check pulse and oxygen levels with an oximeter each morning and evening. A client with worsening symptoms does not go higher, and if symptoms do not improve the group descends.

  ### **The Cho La Crossing**

  - **Who decides:** Your lead guide decides whether the pass is crossed, based on recent snow, wind, visibility and how the group is feeling. Nobody is pushed over it. [LINK: Cho La Pass crossing guide]
  - **Preparation:** We leave Thangnak at 4 to 5 am by headlamp and aim to be over the pass by mid-morning, before the afternoon wind. Bring microspikes, trekking poles, gloves, a warm layer and a hot drink in your daypack.
  - **If the pass is not safe:** The group waits a day at Thangnak and tries again, which uses one of your spare days. If it still cannot be crossed, we turn back down the Gokyo valley to Namche and walk the classic trail via Tengboche and Pheriche to Base Camp and Kala Patthar.

  ### **Permits, Visas & Insurance**

  - **Permits:** You need two mandatory permits: the Sagarmatha National Park Permit and the Khumbu Pasanglhamu Rural Municipality Permit. Both are included in your package. Read more about the [permits for Everest Base Camp](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/permits-for-everest-base-camp-trek/) and [Sagarmatha National Park](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/sagarmatha-national-park/).
  - **Visa:** A Nepal visa can be obtained on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport. Bring a passport valid for at least 6 months, a passport photo, and cash (USD) for the fee. See [things to know before visiting Nepal](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/things-to-know-before-visiting-nepal/).
  - **Travel Insurance:** Comprehensive insurance covering helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 m, medical treatment, and trip cancellation is **mandatory**. _Please note: In the event of a helicopter rescue, you will typically need to pay the operator upfront and claim the cost back from your insurance provider._
  - **Guide & Porter:** Hiring a licensed guide and porter is essential for safety. Porters carry your main duffel (10–12 kg), while you carry a 30–40L daypack. If you are missing any gear, Thamel in Kathmandu has excellent shops to buy or rent equipment, and our guide to [what to pack for a Himalayan trek](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/what-to-pack-for-a-himalayan-trek/) covers the rest.

  ### **Accommodation, Food & Connectivity**

  - **Accommodation:** Teahouses are basic but comfortable, in twin-sharing rooms with shared bathrooms. Thangnak and Dzongla are the most basic, so a sleeping bag liner helps. Hot showers and device charging cost extra, and power can be unreliable, so bring a high-capacity power bank. See what [teahouse rooms and food are really like at different altitudes](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/everest-base-camp-accommodation-and-food/).
  - **Food & Water:** **Breakfast is included every day.** Lunch and dinner are available at teahouses for an additional cost. Drink only boiled or purified water, and bring a reusable bottle and purification tablets to avoid single-use plastic.
  - **Connectivity:** Wi-Fi is available at some teahouses for a fee, but speeds are slow and mobile coverage is limited. Expect to be mostly offline in the Gokyo valley and above Dingboche.

  ### **Money & Logistics**

  - **Currency & Cash:** The local currency is NPR. USD and EUR can be exchanged in Kathmandu and Namche. There are no ATMs above Namche, so carry enough cash for showers, charging, Wi-Fi, snacks, and tips.
  - **Flight Delays:** Lukla flights are weather-dependent and can be delayed. We strongly recommend keeping 1 to 2 spare days after the trek before booking your international flight home. Read [what to expect on the flight to Lukla](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/flight-to-lukla-safety-delays-what-to-expect/).
  - **Seasonal Flights:** During peak seasons, Lukla flights may operate from Manthali/Ramechhap instead of Kathmandu, requiring an early morning drive. Helicopter transfers can be arranged at an additional cost.
  - **Baggage:** Lukla flights have a strict baggage limit, often around 15 kg in total including your daypack. Confirm it before you pack.
  - **Best Seasons:** March–May and September–November. Autumn brings the clearest skies, but nights above 4,000 m fall to about -15 °C. Book 3 to 6 months in advance, avoid the monsoon (roughly June to mid-September), and check the [best time for Everest Base Camp trek](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/best-time-for-everest-base-camp-trek/).

  ### **Mindset & Culture**

  - **Mental Preparation:** Long days, cold nights, and thin air will test your patience. Stay positive, accept a slow pace, and celebrate small milestones.
  - **Respect Local Culture:** Walk clockwise around mani walls and monasteries, and dress modestly at religious sites.
  - **Leave No Trace:** Carry out what you carry in, use biodegradable soap, and avoid single-use plastics. . You can also read our guide to [climate change and trekking in Nepal](https://waveuphimalayas.com/blog/climate-change-nepal-trekking-best-time-to-visit-himalayas/) to understand why protecting the Himalayan environment matters.
