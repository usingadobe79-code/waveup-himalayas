preparation: |-
  ### Best time for an Everest helicopter tour

  - **Autumn (October–December):** the clearest skies and sharpest mountain views of the year. This is our busiest season, so book early.
  - **Spring (March–May):** good visibility, warmer mornings and rhododendron colour on the lower slopes. Afternoon cloud builds up, so we fly early.
  - **Winter (January–February):** cold but often clear. Landings can be icy and windy.
  - **Monsoon (June–August):** the least suitable season, with frequent cloud and delays. Flights run only in clear breaks.
  - **Time of day:** morning flights between 6:00 and 9:00 am are the most reliable, because cloud usually forms later.

  ### Kala Patthar landing and weather

  The Kala Patthar landing is weather permitting and is never guaranteed. Our pilots decide on the day, based on cloud, wind, temperature and the weight of the passengers on board.

  - **If the landing is closed:** we hover and fly over Kala Patthar for photos, overfly Everest Base Camp and still land at Everest View Hotel for breakfast.
  - **If we cannot fly at all:** you can reschedule for another day or take a refund.
  - **If part of the trip is completed:** a partial refund may apply.
  - **Everest Base Camp:** helicopters overfly it. Our landing is at Kala Patthar, which gives a better view of Everest.

  ### Altitude and health

  You fly from Kathmandu (1,400 m) to Lukla (2,860 m), Pheriche (4,371 m) and Kala Patthar (5,545 m) in a few hours, with no time to acclimatize. At 5,545 m the air holds about half the oxygen it does at sea level.

  - **Time at the top:** we keep ground time at Kala Patthar to 5–10 minutes and spend the rest of the flight lower.
  - **Oxygen:** every helicopter carries an emergency oxygen cylinder.
  - **Symptoms to report:** headache, dizziness, nausea, unusual breathlessness or fatigue. Tell your pilot or guide straight away.
  - **Talk to your doctor first** if you have a heart, lung or blood-pressure condition, are pregnant, or have had altitude sickness before.
  - **Look after yourself:** drink water, avoid alcohol the night before and sleep well.
  - **Fitness:** no training is needed.

  ### Insurance

  Travel and medical insurance is mandatory and is not included in the price. Your policy must cover helicopter flights and evacuation up to 5,500 m. Carry the policy number and the emergency phone line with you on the day.

  ### Permits, visa and documents

  - **Permits:** we arrange the permits and paperwork. Airport tax and permits cost about US$100 per person and are paid on top of the package price.
  - **Passport:** bring your passport, or a clear copy, for the flight manifest and permits.
  - **Visa:** most visitors can get a Nepal tourist visa on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport. Check the rule and fee for your passport before you travel.

  ### Weight, baggage and seating

  Helicopters at high altitude have strict weight limits, so we confirm each passenger's weight and baggage before the flight. Pack only a small day bag. Every guest has a window seat, and the pilot arranges seating for balance. Heavier groups may be split across two flights or have the landing shortened.

  ### Who can join

  No trekking experience is needed. Families and older travellers regularly join this tour with their doctor's approval. Ask us about minimum age for children, because it is set by the helicopter operator.

  ### Safety and cancellations

  Flights are operated by experienced high-altitude pilots with oxygen on board. We fly only in stable weather, and the pilot has the final say. Flights can be delayed, rescheduled or rerouted at short notice, so keep your plans for the rest of the day flexible and avoid booking an international flight on the same day.

  ### Booking and payment

  There is no online checkout. Send us your preferred date, group size and Kathmandu hotel, and we confirm availability and send a tailored quote. Tips for the pilot, crew and driver are appreciated but not required.

  ### Responsible travel in the Khumbu

  The Khumbu is the homeland of the Sherpa people and lies inside Sagarmatha National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Take all rubbish back with you, stay with your crew at landing sites and respect local customs and monasteries.
