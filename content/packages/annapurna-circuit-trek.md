itinerarySummary: The Annapurna Circuit Trek is a 14-day guided Himalayan journey through Nepal’s most diverse trekking landscape — from the green Marsyangdi Valley to the dry alpine valleys of Mustang. Starting with a scenic drive from Kathmandu to Dharapani, you’ll trek through traditional villages, cross the legendary Thorong La Pass (5,416 m / 17,769 ft), visit the sacred Muktinath Temple, and descend through the quiet Lupra Valley to Jomsom. With a built-in acclimatization day in Manang, carefully paced daily walks, and expert guides, this moderate-to-strenuous Annapurna Circuit Trek is safe, scenic, and unforgettable.
itineraryHighlights:
  - Cross Thorong La Pass (5,416 m / 17,769 ft) — the highest point and defining achievement of the Annapurna Circuit Trek
  - Experience Nepal’s dramatic landscape change, from the green Marsyangdi Valley to the dry alpine valleys of Manang and Mustang
  - Follow the scenic upper route from Upper Pisang to Ngawal via Gyaru, with wide views of Annapurna II, Annapurna III, Pisang Peak, and the Manang Valley
  - Spend an acclimatization day in Manang (3,540 m / 11,614 ft), with time to visit Gangapurna Lake, Braga Monastery, and viewpoints above the village
  - Visit Muktinath Temple, a sacred Himalayan pilgrimage site at 3,760 m / 12,336 ft, reached after crossing Thorong La Pass
  - Trek through the Lupra Valley, a quieter route to Jomsom with open Mustang scenery, Dhaulagiri views, and Bon cultural heritage
  - Walk through important Annapurna Circuit villages, including Chame, Bhratang, Upper Pisang, Ngawal, Manang, and Muktinath
  - Finish the trek in Jomsom, known for its dramatic Mustang landscapes, apple orchards, and Dhaulagiri views, before flying to Pokhara

faqs:
  - question: How difficult is the Annapurna Circuit Trek?
    answer: The Annapurna Circuit Trek is rated moderate to strenuous. You’ll walk 5–8 hours per day at high altitude, with the crossing of Thorong La Pass (5,416 m) being the most demanding day. No technical climbing skills are required, but good fitness, mental determination, and proper acclimatization are essential. With our 14-day itinerary, the pace is gradual and includes an acclimatization day in Manang to help you adjust safely.
  - question: How many days does the Annapurna Circuit Trek take?
    answer: Our Annapurna Circuit Trek is a 14-day itinerary, including arrival and departure days in Kathmandu. Most trekkers find 12–14 days ideal, as it allows enough time for acclimatization. The Annapurna Circuit trek is commonly completed in 12–14 days of walking, though some longer itineraries extend to 15–20 days.
  - question: What is the best season for the Annapurna Circuit Trek?
    answer: The best seasons are spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November). These months offer stable weather, clear skies, and moderate temperatures ideal for trekking. Spring offers wildflowers and pleasant weather, while autumn brings crisp air and clear vistas of the Annapurna range. Winter treks (December–February) are possible but much colder, and the route usually closes during winter because of heavy snow at Thorong La Pass. The monsoon (June–August) brings rain, clouds, and leeches.
  - question: How high is Thorong La Pass?
    answer: Thorong La Pass sits at 5,416 m (17,769 ft) — the highest point of the Annapurna Circuit Trek. The crossing day typically involves 7–10 hours of walking, starting between 4:00 and 5:30 AM with headlamps. Trekkers who acclimatize properly in Manang, start early, and pace themselves through a 7–9 hour day generally complete the crossing without serious difficulty.
  - question: Do I need travel insurance for the Annapurna Circuit Trek?
    answer: Yes — comprehensive travel insurance is mandatory. Your policy must cover high-altitude trekking, helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 m, medical treatment, and trip cancellation. We’ll ask for your policy details before departure.
  - question: What permits are required for the Annapurna Circuit Trek?
    answer: Only the Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) is required for the Annapurna Circuit Trek — no local municipality permit is needed. The permit costs approximately USD 30 (NPR 3,000) for foreign nationals and is valid for up to one month. WaveUp Himalayas handles all paperwork for you.
  - question: How fit do I need to be for the Annapurna Circuit Trek?
    answer: You should be in good physical shape and able to walk 5–8 hours per day with a light daypack. We recommend starting cardio and strength training 8–12 weeks before your trek, including hill walks with a loaded backpack. Prior trekking experience is helpful but not required.
  - question: What is the risk of altitude sickness on the Annapurna Circuit Trek?
    answer: Altitude sickness (AMS) is a real risk above 3,000 m. Our itinerary includes an acclimatization day in Manang (3,540 m) and a “climb high, sleep low” approach. Your guide will monitor your oxygen levels daily with an oximeter. If symptoms worsen, the only safe treatment is to descend immediately.
  - question: What kind of accommodation is available during the trek?
    answer: You’ll stay in teahouses (mountain lodges) in twin-sharing rooms with shared bathrooms. Rooms are basic but comfortable, with blankets provided. Attached bathroom accommodation is available except at Yak Kharka and Thorong Phedi. Hot showers, Wi-Fi, and device charging cost extra above Chame. In Kathmandu and Pokhara, you’ll stay in 3-star hotels with breakfast included.
  - question: What food and water is available on the Annapurna Circuit Trek?
    answer: Breakfast is included every day, and a farewell dinner is included in Kathmandu. Lunch and dinner during the trek are not included — you can order directly from teahouse menus and pay locally. Menus offer Nepali staples like dal bhat, plus noodles, pasta, soups, and eggs. Drink only boiled, filtered, or purified water — avoid untreated tap water. We recommend carrying a reusable bottle and purification tablets.

  - question: Can I do the Annapurna Circuit Trek solo?
    answer: Yes — many trekkers join our group departures as solo travelers, and you’ll share a room with another trekker of the same gender. We also offer private Annapurna Circuit Treks for solo trekkers who prefer their own guide and flexible pace.
  - question: Is there an age limit for the Annapurna Circuit Trek?
    answer: There’s no strict age limit, but trekkers should be in good health. We’ve guided travelers from their late teens to their 70s. Anyone with heart, lung, or blood pressure conditions should consult a doctor before booking. Annapurna Circuit Trek for seniors is available with a slower, customized itinerary.
  - question: What happens if Jomsom flights are delayed or cancelled?
    answer: Jomsom flights are weather-dependent and can be delayed or cancelled due to strong winds or poor visibility. We recommend keeping 1–2 spare days after your trek. In such cases, WaveUp Himalayas will arrange alternative road transportation (6–7 hours) to Pokhara at no additional cost.
  - question: How much weight can I carry, and do you provide porters?
    answer: Porters carry your main duffel bag (max 10–12 kg), while you carry a daypack with essentials. We provide 1 porter for every 2 trekkers as standard. Extra porters can be arranged on request.
  - question: Do I need a visa for Nepal?
    answer: Most nationalities can obtain a Nepal visa on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport. Bring a passport valid for at least 6 months, a passport photo, and the visa fee in cash (USD or major currencies).
  - question: Should I tip my guide and porters?
    answer: Tipping is customary and greatly appreciated. As a guideline, budget USD 10–15 per day for your guide and USD 8–10 per day for porters, shared among the group.

preparation: |-
  - **Start Physical Training Early** – Begin cardio and strength training 8–12 weeks before your trek. Focus on walking, stairs, squats, and lunges.
  - **Break In Your Trekking Boots** – Wear your boots for at least 2–3 weeks before departure to avoid blisters and foot pain.
  - **Arrange Travel Insurance** – Comprehensive insurance covering helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 m, medical treatment, and trip cancellation.
  - **Get a Health Check-Up –** Consult your doctor, complete vaccinations, and ask about altitude sickness medication like Diamox.
  - **Prepare Your Packing List** – Aim for 10–12 kg base pack weight. Bring layers, down jacket, sleeping bag, poles, headlamp, sunscreen, and personal medical kit.
  - **Carry Enough Cash** – There are no ATMs above Chame. Carry Nepali Rupees for hot showers, charging, Wi-Fi, snacks, drinks, and tips.
  - **Train on Hills with a Backpack** – Do practice hikes with a loaded pack on consecutive days to prepare for 5–8 hours of daily walking.
  - **Learn About Altitude Sickness** – Know the symptoms (headache, nausea, dizziness), ascend slowly, hydrate, and descend immediately if symptoms worsen.
  - **Mental Preparation** – The Annapurna Circuit Trek is as much a mental challenge as a physical one. Stay positive and embrace the journey.
  - **Respect Local Culture** – Learn basic Nepali phrases, respect local traditions, and walk clockwise around mani walls and monasteries.
  - **Leave No Trace** – Carry out what you carry in. Use biodegradable products and avoid single-use plastics.
  - **Book in Advance** – Peak seasons (March–May, September–November) fill up quickly. Book your Annapurna Circuit Trek package at least 3–6 months in advance.
