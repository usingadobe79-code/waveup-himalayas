faqs:
  - question: How difficult is the Everest Base Camp Trek?
    answer: The Everest Base Camp Trek is rated moderate to challenging. You'll walk 5–8 hours per day at high altitude, with steep ascents and descents. No technical climbing skills are required, but good fitness, mental determination, and proper acclimatization are essential. With our 13-day itinerary, the pace is gradual and includes acclimatization days to help you adjust safely.
  - question: How many days does the Everest Base Camp Trek take?
    answer: Our Everest Base Camp Trek is a 13-day itinerary, including arrival and departure days in Kathmandu. Most trekkers find 12–14 days ideal, as it allows enough time for acclimatization. Shorter versions are possible by helicopter return from Gorak Shep or by skipping rest days.
  - question: What is the best season for the Everest Base Camp Trek?
    answer: The best seasons are spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November). Spring offers warmer temperatures and blooming rhododendrons, while autumn brings clear skies and stable weather. Winter treks (December–February) are possible but much colder, and the monsoon (June–August) brings rain, clouds, and flight delays.
  - question: How high is Everest Base Camp and Kala Patthar?
    answer: Everest Base Camp sits at 5,364 m (17,598 ft), and Kala Patthar reaches 5,555 m (18,225 ft) — the highest point of the trek. Most trekkers find Kala Patthar more rewarding for views, as Everest itself is not visible from Base Camp.
  - question: Do I need travel insurance for the Everest Base Camp Trek?
    answer: Yes — comprehensive travel insurance is mandatory. Your policy must cover high-altitude trekking up to at least 6,000 m, helicopter evacuation, medical treatment, and trip cancellation. We'll ask for your policy details before departure.
  - question: What permits are required for the Everest Base Camp Trek?
    answer: 'You need two permits: the Sagarmatha National Park Permit and the Khumbu Pasanglhamu Rural Municipality Permit. Both are included in your Wave Up Himalayas package — we handle all paperwork for you.'
  - question: How fit do I need to be for the Everest Base Camp Trek?
    answer: You should be in good physical shape and able to walk 5–8 hours per day with a light daypack. We recommend starting cardio and strength training 8–12 weeks before your trek, including hill walks with a loaded backpack. Prior trekking experience is helpful but not required.
  - question: What is the risk of altitude sickness on the Everest Base Camp Trek?
    answer: Altitude sickness (AMS) is a real risk above 3,000 m. Our itinerary includes two acclimatization days (Namche and Dingboche) and a "climb high, sleep low" approach. Your guide will monitor your oxygen levels daily with an oximeter. If symptoms worsen, the only safe treatment is to descend immediately.
  - question: What kind of accommodation is available during the trek?
    answer: You'll stay in teahouses (mountain lodges) in twin-sharing rooms with shared bathrooms. Rooms are basic but comfortable, with blankets provided. Hot showers, Wi-Fi, and device charging cost extra above Namche. In Kathmandu, you'll stay in a 3-star hotel with breakfast included.
  - question: What food and water is available on the Everest Base Camp Trek?
    answer: Three meals a day (breakfast, lunch, dinner) are included during trekking days. Menus offer Nepali staples like dal bhat, plus noodles, pasta, soups, and eggs. Drink only boiled, filtered, or purified water — avoid untreated tap water. We recommend carrying a reusable bottle and purification tablets.

  - question: Can I do the Everest Base Camp Trek solo?
    answer: Yes — many trekkers join our group departures as solo travelers, and you'll share a room with another trekker of the same gender. We also offer private Everest Base Camp Treks for solo trekkers who prefer their own guide and flexible pace.
  - question: Is there an age limit for the Everest Base Camp Trek?
    answer: There's no strict age limit, but trekkers should be in good health. We've guided travelers from their late teens to their 70s. Anyone with heart, lung, or blood pressure conditions should consult a doctor before booking. Everest Base Camp Trek for seniors is available with a slower, customized itinerary.
  - question: What happens if Lukla flights are delayed or cancelled?
    answer: Lukla flights are weather-dependent and can be delayed or cancelled. We recommend keeping 1–2 spare days after your trek. In peak seasons, flights may operate from Manthali/Ramechhap instead of Kathmandu, requiring an early morning drive. Helicopter transfers can be arranged at an additional cost.
  - question: How much weight can I carry, and do you provide porters?
    answer: Porters carry your main duffel bag (max 10–12 kg), while you carry a daypack with essentials. We provide 1 porter for every 2 trekkers as standard. Extra porters can be arranged on request.
  - question: Do I need a visa for Nepal?
    answer: Most nationalities can obtain a Nepal visa on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport. Bring a passport valid for at least 6 months, a passport photo, and the visa fee in cash (USD or major currencies).
  - question: Should I tip my guide and porters?
    answer: Tipping is customary and greatly appreciated. As a guideline, budget USD 10–15 per day for your guide and USD 8–10 per day for porters, shared among the group. Tips should be given at the end of the trek.
  - question: Is Wi-Fi and mobile coverage available on the trek?
    answer: Wi-Fi is available at most teahouses for a fee, but speeds are slow. Mobile coverage (Ncell/NTC) works up to Tengboche and is limited beyond that. Everest Link cards offer internet at higher villages. Expect to be mostly offline above Dingboche.

preparation: |-
  - **Start Physical Training Early** – Begin cardio and strength training 8–12 weeks before your trek. Focus on walking, stairs, squats, and lunges. High-altitude trekking demands more than flat-land walking.
  - **Break In Your Trekking Boots** – Wear your boots for at least 2–3 weeks before departure to avoid blisters and foot pain.
  - **Arrange Travel Insurance** – Mandatory insurance covering helicopter evacuation up to 6,000 m, medical treatment, and trip cancellation.
  - **Get a Health Check-Up** – Consult your doctor, complete vaccinations, and ask about altitude sickness medication like Diamox.
  - **Prepare Your Packing List** – Aim for 10–11 kg base pack weight. Bring layers, down jacket, sleeping bag, poles, headlamp, sunscreen, and personal medical kit.
  - **Carry Enough Cash** – There are no ATMs above Namche. Carry Nepali Rupees for hot showers, charging, Wi-Fi, snacks, drinks, and tips.
  - **Train on Hills with a Backpack** – Do practice hikes with a loaded pack on consecutive days to prepare for 5–8 hours of daily walking at altitude.
  - **Learn About Altitude Sickness** – Know the symptoms (headache, nausea, dizziness), ascend slowly, hydrate, and descend immediately if symptoms worsen.
  - **Mental Preparation** – The EBC Trek is as much a mental challenge as a physical one. Stay positive, be patient, and embrace the journey.
  - **Respect Local Culture** – Learn basic Nepali phrases, respect Sherpa traditions, and walk clockwise around mani walls and monasteries.
  - **Leave No Trace** – Carry out what you carry in. Use biodegradable products and avoid single-use plastics.
  - **Book in Advance** – Peak seasons (March–May, September–November) fill up quickly. Book your Everest Base Camp Trek package at least 3–6 months in advance.
