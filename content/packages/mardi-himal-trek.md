faqs:
  - question: How difficult is the Mardi Himal Trek?
    answer: The Mardi Himal Trek is rated easy to moderate. You'll walk 4–7 hours per day, with some steep sections above Badal Danda. No technical climbing skills are required, and the trek is suitable for beginners with reasonable fitness. The highest point is the Upper Viewpoint at 4,200 m.
  - question: How many days does the Mardi Himal Trek take?
    answer: Our Mardi Himal Trek is a 9-day itinerary, including arrival and departure days in Kathmandu. The trekking portion itself is 5 days. Shorter versions are possible by starting from Pokhara directly.
  - question: What is the best season for the Mardi Himal Trek?
    answer: The best seasons are spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November). Spring offers blooming rhododendrons and warmer temperatures, while autumn brings clear skies and stable weather. October and November offer the clearest mountain views of the year.
  - question: How high is the Mardi Himal Upper Viewpoint?
    answer: The Mardi Himal Upper Viewpoint sits at approximately 4,200 m (13,780 ft). The full Mardi Himal Base Camp is at 4,500 m, which is an optional extension from the Upper Viewpoint.
  - question: Do I need travel insurance for the Mardi Himal Trek?
    answer: Yes—comprehensive travel insurance is strongly recommended. Your policy should cover helicopter evacuation up to 4,500 m, medical treatment, and trip cancellation. We'll ask for your policy details before departure.
  - question: What permits are required for the Mardi Himal Trek?
    answer: "You need two permits: the Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) and a TIMS card (Trekkers' Information Management System). Both are mandatory. ACAP costs NPR 3,000 (approximately USD 22–25) and TIMS costs NPR 2,000 (approximately USD 13–20) for foreign trekkers. Both are included in your Waveup Himalayas package—we handle all paperwork for you."
  - question: How fit do I need to be for the Mardi Himal Trek?
    answer: You should be in reasonable physical shape and able to walk 4–7 hours per day with a light daypack. We recommend starting cardio and strength training 6–8 weeks before your trek, including hill walks with a loaded backpack. Prior trekking experience is helpful but not required.
  - question: What is the risk of altitude sickness on the Mardi Himal Trek?
    answer: Altitude sickness (AMS) is a risk above 3,000 m. Our itinerary includes gradual ascent to help your body adjust. Your guide will monitor your oxygen levels daily with an oximeter. If symptoms worsen, the only safe treatment is to descend immediately.
  - question: What kind of accommodation is available during the trek?
    answer: You'll stay in teahouses (mountain lodges) in twin-sharing rooms with shared bathrooms. Rooms are basic but comfortable, with blankets provided. Hot showers, Wi-Fi, and device charging cost extra above Forest Camp. In Kathmandu and Pokhara, you'll stay in 3-star hotels with breakfast included.
  - question: What food and water is available on the Mardi Himal Trek?
    answer: Breakfast is included every day during the trek and in cities. Lunch and dinner are available at teahouses for an additional cost. Menus offer Nepali staples like dal bhat, plus noodles, pasta, soups, and eggs. Drink only boiled, filtered, or purified water. Plastic bottled water is not permitted on the Mardi Himal route—bring a reusable bottle and purification tablets.
  - question: How much does the Mardi Himal Trek cost?
    answer: The cost depends on group size, season, and services included. Our Mardi Himal Trek package covers permits, transportation, accommodation, breakfast, guide, and porters. Contact Waveup Himalayas for a customized quote for 2026 or 2027 departures.
  - question: Can I do the Mardi Himal Trek solo?
    answer: Yes—many trekkers join our group departures as solo travelers, and you'll share a room with another trekker of the same gender. We also offer private Mardi Himal Treks for solo trekkers who prefer their own guide and flexible pace.
  - question: Is there an age limit for the Mardi Himal Trek?
    answer: There's no strict age limit, but trekkers should be in good health. We've guided travelers from their late teens to their 70s. Anyone with heart, lung, or blood pressure conditions should consult a doctor before booking.
  - question: How much weight can I carry, and do you provide porters?
    answer: Porters carry your main duffel bag (max 10–12 kg), while you carry a daypack with essentials. We provide 1 porter for every 2 trekkers as standard. Extra porters can be arranged on request.
  - question: Do I need a visa for Nepal?
    answer: Most nationalities can obtain a Nepal visa on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport. Bring a passport valid for at least 6 months, a passport photo, and the visa fee in cash (USD or major currencies).
  - question: Should I tip my guide and porters?
    answer: Tipping is customary and greatly appreciated. As a guideline, budget USD 10–15 per day for your guide and USD 8–10 per day for porters, shared among the group.

preparation: |-
  - **Start Physical Training Early** – Begin cardio and strength training 6–8 weeks before your trek. Focus on walking, stairs, squats, and lunges.
  - **Break In Your Trekking Boots** – Wear your boots for at least 2–3 weeks before departure to avoid blisters and foot pain.
  - **Arrange Travel Insurance** – Comprehensive insurance covering helicopter evacuation up to 4,500 m, medical treatment, and trip cancellation.
  - **Get a Health Check-Up** – Consult your doctor, complete vaccinations, and ask about altitude sickness medication like Diamox.
  - **Prepare Your Packing List** – Aim for 8–10 kg base pack weight. Bring layers, down jacket, sleeping bag, poles, headlamp, sunscreen, and personal medical kit.
  - **Carry Enough Cash** – There are no ATMs above Pokhara. Carry Nepali Rupees for hot showers, charging, Wi-Fi, snacks, drinks, and tips.
  - **Train on Hills with a Backpack** – Do practice hikes with a loaded pack on consecutive days to prepare for 4–7 hours of daily walking.
  - **Learn About Altitude Sickness** – Know the symptoms (headache, nausea, dizziness), ascend slowly, hydrate, and descend immediately if symptoms worsen.
  - **Mental Preparation** – The Mardi Himal Trek is as much a mental challenge as a physical one. Stay positive and embrace the journey.
  - **Respect Local Culture** – Learn basic Nepali phrases, respect Gurung traditions, and walk clockwise around mani walls and monasteries.
  - **Leave No Trace** – Carry out what you carry in. Use biodegradable products and avoid single-use plastics.
  - **Book in Advance** – Peak seasons (March–May, September–November) fill up quickly. Book your Mardi Himal Trek package at least 2–3 months in advance.
