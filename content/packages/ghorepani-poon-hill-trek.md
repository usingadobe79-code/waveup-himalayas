faqs:
  - question: How difficult is the Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek?
    answer: The Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek is easy to moderate. You'll walk 4–6 hours per day, with the toughest section being the 3,300 stone steps from Tikhedhunga to Ulleri. No technical climbing is required, and the trek suits beginners, families and trekkers of all ages.
  - question: How many days does the Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek take?
    answer: Our standard Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek itinerary is 8 days including arrival and departure in Kathmandu. The trekking portion can be done in 4–5 days, and we also offer a Poon Hill Trek 5 days, a Poon Hill Trek 4 days and a Poon Hill Trek 3 days version for travellers with limited time.
  - question: What is the best time to do the Poon Hill Trek?
    answer: The best seasons are autumn (October–November) for the clearest mountain views and spring (March–May) for blooming rhododendrons. Winter treks are possible with proper gear, while the monsoon months are best avoided.
  - question: How high is Poon Hill?
    answer: Poon Hill sits at 3,210 m (10,531 ft) — the highest point of the trek. The sunrise view from the summit is the main highlight, offering a 180-degree panorama of Dhaulagiri, Annapurna South, Machhapuchhre and Nilgiri.
  - question: Do I need travel insurance for the Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek?
    answer: Yes. We strongly recommend comprehensive travel insurance covering trekking up to 3,500 m, helicopter evacuation, medical treatment and trip cancellation. You'll be asked to provide your policy details before departure.
  - question: What permits do I need for the Poon Hill Trek?
    answer: You need the Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) at NPR 3,000 and the TIMS card at NPR 2,000. Both are included in your WaveUp Himalayas package and arranged on your behalf.
  - question: How fit do I need to be for the Poon Hill Trek?
    answer: You should be able to walk 4–6 hours a day with a light daypack. We recommend starting cardio and strength training 4–6 weeks before your trek. Prior trekking experience is helpful but not required.
  - question: What kind of accommodation is available on the trek?
    answer: You'll stay in teahouses in twin-sharing rooms with shared bathrooms. Rooms are basic but comfortable and blankets are provided. Hot showers, Wi-Fi and charging cost extra. In Kathmandu and Pokhara you'll stay in a 3-star hotel with breakfast included.
  - question: What meals are included in the Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek package?
    answer: Your package includes breakfast every day and a farewell dinner in Kathmandu. Lunch and dinner on trekking days are not included — you'll order directly from teahouse menus, which is the most flexible and affordable option.
  - question: How much does the Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek cost?
    answer: The cost depends on group size, season and service level. Our package covers permits, guide, porter, accommodation, breakfast, transport and the farewell dinner. Contact WaveUp Himalayas for a customized quote for 2026 or 2027 departures.
  - question: Can I do the Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek solo?
    answer: Yes. Many trekkers join our group departures as solo travellers and share a room with another trekker of the same gender. We also offer private Poon Hill Treks for solo trekkers who prefer their own guide and flexible pacing.
  - question: Is there an age limit for the Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek?
    answer: There's no strict age limit, but trekkers should be in good health. We've guided travellers from young children to seniors in their seventies. Anyone with heart, lung or blood pressure conditions should consult a doctor first.
  - question: Do I need a visa for Nepal?
    answer: Most nationalities can obtain a Nepal visa on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport. Bring a passport valid for at least six months, a passport photo and the visa fee in cash (USD or major currencies).
  - question: Should I tip my guide and porters?
    answer: Tipping is customary and greatly appreciated. As a guideline, budget USD 5–10 per day for your guide and USD 5–8 per day for porters, shared among the group. Tips are given at the end of the trek.
  - question: Is Wi-Fi and mobile coverage available on the trek?
    answer: Wi-Fi is available at most teahouses for a fee, though speeds are slow. Mobile coverage (Ncell/NTC) is intermittent along the trail. Expect to be mostly offline on trekking days.
  - question: What is the weather like on the Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek?
    answer: Weather varies significantly by season. In autumn (October-November), expect clear skies, daytime temperatures of 15-20°C, and cold nights at Ghorepani where temperatures can drop to -8°C or lower. In spring (March-May), days are warmer (15-22°C) with blooming rhododendrons, but mornings can carry haze. Winter (December-February) brings genuinely cold mornings - the pre-dawn Poon Hill climb can be between -10°C and 0°C - but skies are often exceptionally clear and the trail is quiet. The monsoon (June-August) brings daily rain, leeches, and cloud cover that regularly blocks the sunrise view, making it the one season best avoided.
  - question: Can children and families do the Ghorepani Poon Hill Trek?
    answer: Yes, great for families. Suitable for children 5–7+ with good fitness. Itinerary can be adjusted for shorter days.
  - question: What happens if I need to turn back or evacuate during the trek?
    answer: Guide will assess and arrange descent with porter or assistant. Helicopter evacuation coordinated via your insurance.

preparation: |-
  - **Start training 4–6 weeks out** — focus on cardio, stair climbing and squats
  - **Break in your boots** at least two weeks before departure to avoid blisters
  - **Do practice hikes** on consecutive days with a loaded daypack
  - **Arrange comprehensive travel insurance** covering helicopter evacuation, medical treatment and trip cancellation up to 3,500 m
  - **Consult your doctor** about altitude medication and any existing conditions
  - **Learn the symptoms of altitude sickness** — headache, nausea, dizziness. Ascend slowly, hydrate, and descend if symptoms worsen
  - **Carry enough cash** — there are ATMs in Pokhara but none on the trail
  - **Respect local culture** — remove shoes before entering homes, walk clockwise around many walls, and ask before photographing people
  - **Leave no trace** — carry out what you carry in and avoid single-use plastics
  - **Book early** — October, November, March and April departures fill up fast
