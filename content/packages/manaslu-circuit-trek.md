faqs:
  - question: "How difficult is the Manaslu Circuit Trek?"
    answer: "It's a moderately challenging trek. You'll walk 5–7 hours a day on remote mountain trails and cross the Larkya La Pass at 5,106 m, so good fitness and proper acclimatisation matter. No technical climbing is required."
  - question: "How many days do you need for the Manaslu Circuit Trek?"
    answer: "The classic itinerary is around 14 days, including travel to and from Kathmandu. Shorter and longer versions — with side trips like Tsum Valley or Manaslu Base Camp — can be arranged."
  - question: "What is the best time for the Manaslu Circuit Trek?"
    answer: "Autumn (September–November) and spring (March–May) give the clearest skies, stable weather and the best mountain views. Winter is very cold at altitude and the monsoon (June–August) brings rain and leeches."
  - question: "Do I need permits for the Manaslu Circuit Trek?"
    answer: "Yes. Manaslu is a restricted area, so you need a Restricted Area Permit (RAP) plus MCAP and ACAP, and you must trek with a licensed guide and at least two trekkers. We arrange all of this for you."
  - question: "How high is the Larkya La Pass?"
    answer: "The Larkya La Pass is the highest point of the trek at about 5,106 m (16,752 ft). It's a long, demanding day, which is why the itinerary builds in acclimatisation beforehand."
  - question: "Is the Manaslu Circuit Trek safe for beginners?"
    answer: "With good preparation it's achievable for fit first-time trekkers, but it isn't a beginner's stroll — the altitude and daily distances are real. We recommend some hiking experience and a few weeks of cardio training beforehand."

preparation: |-
  Manaslu is a high-altitude trek with long days, so a few weeks of preparation makes a big difference.

  ## Build your fitness (start 6–8 weeks ahead)
  - **Cardio:** running, cycling, swimming or brisk hiking, 3–4 times a week.
  - **Strength:** squats, lunges and core work for the long climbs and descents.
  - **Hike with a loaded pack:** practise on hills carrying your daypack.

  ## Prepare for altitude
  - The itinerary builds in gradual ascent and acclimatisation — follow it, don't rush.
  - Stay hydrated, keep a steady pace, and tell your guide early if you feel unwell.
  - Ask your doctor about altitude medication (such as Diamox) before you travel.

  ## Mindset
  - Days are long and remote, with basic teahouse comforts. Come ready for a real adventure — the rewards are huge.
